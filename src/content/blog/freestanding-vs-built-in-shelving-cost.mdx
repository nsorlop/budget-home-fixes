A freestanding bookcase costs anywhere from about $25 for a small particleboard unit to about $350 for a solid pine one at IKEA's US prices in September 2026. A built-in wall of shelves built by a carpenter is a different order of money: most 2026 guides land between roughly $1,000 and $5,000, and one big national average sits near $2,850. Wall-mounted floating shelves sit in between.

Cost is only part of it. For renters the real questions are what you leave behind when you move, how many holes go in the wall, and whether a tall bookcase can tip. This guide compares prices, shows where the cost guides disagree, and ends with a cost-per-year comparison we calculated ourselves.

<Callout type="takeaway">
- **Freestanding:** about $23–$349 per unit at IKEA US (September 2026); it moves with you, but tall units should be anchored to a stud.
- **Floating shelves:** one 43-inch IKEA shelf is $29.99 with a stated 22 lb maximum load; heavier loads need studs.
- **Built-in, hired out:** roughly $1,000–$5,000 for a wall in most 2026 guides; per-foot figures range from about $65 to over $500.
- **Built-in, DIY:** about $400–$1,200 in materials and around 8 hours for a plywood bookcase project.
- **Over 10 years,** our arithmetic puts a freestanding pair at about $17 a year and a professional built-in at $150–$300 a year, and the built-in stays with the house.
</Callout>

### Wall-mounted (floating) shelves

A single IKEA LACK shelf, 43¼ inches long and 10¼ inches deep, is $29.99. IKEA lists its maximum load at 22 lb, and the screws for the wall are sold separately. Builds and Buys (January 2026) prices professionally installed floating shelves at $75–$175 per linear foot, with $25–$75 of that in materials.

## Why built-ins cost so much more

Labor. Bob Vila says labor is 70 to 80 percent of a project budget, while Ash Deco says about half and Davies Decor says 40–60 percent. Hourly rates vary too: $30–$130 (Bob Vila), $66–$121 for a finish carpenter (Fixr) and $50–$85 (AskDoss, which estimates $1,200–$3,400 of labor for a full project).

DIY changes the math, but not to freestanding levels. This Old House (updated April 2026) rates its plywood bookcase build, a general bookcase guide rather than a built-in one, as moderate difficulty, about 8 hours, with $500–$1,200 of wood plus $24–$50 of hardware. A 2020 blog post about two 48-inch built-ins beside a bunk bed lists about $325 of plywood, and the author screwed them into the wall studs.

## Weight, anchoring and tip-over safety

Freestanding does not mean unattached. The CPSC recommends anchoring bookcases and other furniture to the wall, and says to keep heavy items on lower shelves. The National Council on Aging reports about 17,800 emergency-room visits a year from tip-overs (2020–2022 data) and at least 608 deaths between 2000 and July 2023, about 71 percent of them children. It says an anchor kit costs under $20 and takes about 20 minutes to fit, with screws going into studs rather than drywall alone and metal brackets preferred over plastic.

Consumer Reports found that some anchor kits which resisted more than 300 pounds at a stud gave out below 60 pounds in hollow drywall, and that most kits cost under $15. It also notes the January 2024 recall of millions of zip-tie anchor kits that could turn brittle. The mandatory federal furniture standard that took effect in September 2023 covers clothing storage units, so do not assume a bookcase came with hardware.

Wall-mounted shelves have the opposite problem: their limit is the fixings. One floating-shelf retailer, Shelfology, says a bracket holds about 45–50 lb per stud it is screwed into, and that a deeper shelf reduces the capacity. Ash Deco's guide lists 5–10 lb for plastic anchors in drywall and 25–50 lb for toggle bolts, against 25–100 lb into studs depending on the mount. Both are shelf sellers and neither cites lab tests, so treat the figures as rough. IKEA's own figure for the LACK is 22 lb.

<Callout type="warning" title="Anchor tall bookcases even in a rental">
A stud anchor leaves small screw holes. A tip-over can injure someone. Weigh the two, and if you are unsure about your lease, ask the landlord in writing.
</Callout>

## Wall damage and your deposit

A freestanding unit puts no holes in the wall, apart from the anchor above. One landlord-law guide (August 2023) says small nail holes from hanging pictures are normal wear and tear, while large holes from drilling, clusters of holes and damage that cracks the paint can be charged. It says flat fees of $5–$25 per hole are typical, or actual repair cost. That guide does not cover state differences, and rules vary by state and by lease, so this is not legal advice. Our deposit checklist has more on [getting your full security deposit back](/blog/get-your-full-security-deposit-back).

Built-ins are the riskiest for renters: they use many anchor points and usually trim work, so repairs are bigger. Builds and Buys says built-ins rarely justify the cost in rentals, citing tenant damage risk and limited flexibility in arranging furniture. If a landlord does approve one in writing, remember it stays when you go.

If you want a shelf with no drilling at all, adhesive strips are the route. House Digest points to Command strips rated for 16 lb, warns that the shelf and everything on it must stay under the strip rating, and says to clean the wall first and wait a week on fresh paint. See our guide to [Command hooks vs. nails and screws](/blog/command-hooks-vs-nails-screws) for what they will and will not hold.

## Cost per year: a worked example

The numbers below are our own arithmetic, not from a source. Assume one wall of book storage about 5 to 6 feet wide and about 80 inches tall. For freestanding, two tall 31½-inch BILLY units at $79 each ($158) plus a $15 anchor kit (a figure from the Consumer Reports and NCOA ranges) is about $173. For wall-mounted, four 43-inch LACK shelves at $29.99 is about $120, plus $20 for screws and anchors that we assumed. Built-in figures use the ranges above: DIY $400–$1,200 and hired-out $1,500–$3,000.

| Option | Upfront | Per year over 3 years | Per year over 10 years |
|---|---|---|---|
| Two freestanding BILLY units + anchors | about $173 | about $58 | about $17 |
| Four LACK wall shelves + hardware | about $140 | about $47 | about $14 |
| DIY built-in | $400–$1,200 | $133–$400 | $40–$120 |
| Built-in, hired out | $1,500–$3,000 | $500–$1,000 | $150–$300 |

This assumes the freestanding units and shelves come with you and stay useful, and that the built-in is left behind, which is what happens in a rental. It ignores your time on DIY, resale value and the difference in capacity: four LACK shelves at 22 lb each hold far less than two bookcases. Put another way, one hired-out built-in at $1,500 costs about 8.7 times our freestanding pair. Freestanding at $58 a year over three years beats every built-in row, and even at 10 years it is cheaper.

## Small spaces and awkward alcoves

Freestanding units cost floor area. A 31½-inch BILLY is 11 inches deep, which works out to about 2.4 square feet of floor per unit (our arithmetic). Wall shelves free the floor and suit a studio or narrow hall, though they are limited by the studs you can find.

Built-ins make the most sense where no stock unit fits: an odd alcove, a wall with a window in the middle, or a sloped ceiling. AskDoss puts a basic alcove at $800–$1,500, which is a lot for a rental but reasonable for an owner. ## When each option makes sense

| Your situation | Best fit | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Renting, may move in 1–3 years | Freestanding, anchored to a stud | Lowest cost per year and it moves with you |
| Renting, light decor only | Adhesive strips or a few small wall shelves | Removable, low risk if kept under the rating |
| Renting, heavy books | Freestanding, heavy items on low shelves | Avoids weak drywall fixings |
| Owning, staying 10+ years | Built-in or DIY built-in | Cost per year falls and it can fit the wall exactly |
| Owning, odd alcove or window wall | Built-in | Stock units leave gaps |
| Owning and possibly selling soon | Freestanding | Sources disagree on resale value |

On resale, AskDoss claims a 50–70 percent return for custom built-ins, while HomeLight (2021) quotes an appraiser who says a single upgrade like this is hard to value and depends on the home and the neighborhood. Do not count on getting the money back.

Choose freestanding if you rent, might move soon, or want to reconfigure. Choose wall shelves if floor space is the problem and the studs line up. Choose a built-in if you own, will stay for years, and the space is awkward enough that a carpenter's time buys real fit. For more renter-safe options, see [renter-friendly upgrades with prices](/blog/renter-friendly-upgrades-with-prices).

## Shelving cost FAQ

### Is it cheaper to buy freestanding shelves or build them in?
Up front, by a wide margin: $23–$349 per unit at IKEA versus about $400–$1,200 for DIY built-ins and $1,000–$5,000 hired out. Over 10 years the per-year gap shrinks, but our arithmetic still favors freestanding.

### Can a renter install built-in shelves?
Only with the landlord's approval, ideally in writing. The shelves stay behind, and damage beyond small holes can come out of your deposit. Builds and Buys steers renters toward freestanding furniture.

### Do freestanding bookcases need to be anchored to the wall?
The CPSC recommends it for bookcases and dressers. Use screws long enough to reach a wood stud and metal brackets; the National Council on Aging says a kit costs under $20 and takes about 20 minutes. Anchoring to drywall alone is much weaker.

### How much weight can a floating shelf hold?
It depends on the mount. IKEA rates its 43-inch LACK at 22 lb. Shelfology says about 45–50 lb per stud in a heavy-duty bracket, and Command strips are rated up to 16 lb total for the shelf and its contents.

### Do built-in bookshelves add resale value?
Sources disagree. One guide claims 50–70 percent of the cost back for custom work; another source, quoting an appraiser, says value depends on the home and is hard to isolate. Treat any return as a bonus.
