Adhesive hooks look more expensive per unit than a box of nails, so it's easy to assume drilling is the "cheap" option. Once you add the move-out cost, the math flips for most renters.

## Sticker price

| | Adhesive/no-drill hooks | Nails/screws |
|---|---|---|
| Cost per hook/anchor | $1.50–$4 each | $0.10–$0.50 each |
| Tools needed | None (mount included) | Hammer or drill ($0–$40 if you don't own one) |
| Cost for 10 wall points | $15–$40 | $1–$5 (plus tool cost if buying one) |

On price alone, nails and screws win by a wide margin — often 5–10x cheaper per hole.

## What it actually costs at move-out

This is the number most people skip:

- **Nail/screw holes**: landlords commonly charge $10–$25 per hole to patch and repaint the wall section, or deduct a flat $50–$150 from the deposit if there are multiple holes across a unit.
- **Adhesive hooks (used correctly)**: rated for their listed weight and removed by pulling the tab slowly, most leave no mark. Failure mode is usually paint pulling off with the adhesive on cheap or poorly-cured paint — call it a 10–15% chance of minor touch-up needed, not guaranteed damage.

## Real total cost example

Say you're hanging 10 items around a one-bedroom apartment:

| | Upfront cost | Expected move-out cost | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adhesive hooks | $25 | ~$0–$10 (rare touch-up) | **$25–$35** |
| Nails/screws | $3 | $100–$250 (patch + repaint 10 holes) | **$103–$253** |

The upfront savings from nails almost never survives contact with a real move-out inspection.

## When nails/screws are still worth it

- You own the property, or your lease explicitly allows wall modifications.
- You're hanging something heavy (over 15–25 lbs, depending on the hook) where adhesive ratings fall short — a heavy mirror or a shelf with real weight on it is not a place to cut corners.
- You're staying multiple years and would rather fix a handful of holes once than manage adhesive residue over time.

## Bottom line

For anything under the weight limit of a rated adhesive hook, the no-drill option is cheaper once you account for the deposit — not just cheaper to buy. Save nails and screws for permanent installs or heavier items where the adhesive rating genuinely isn't enough.
