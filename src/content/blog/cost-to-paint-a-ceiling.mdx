Hiring a painter for a plain, flat ceiling runs about $1.37–$3 per square foot in the 2026 guides we could read, which works out to roughly $200–$500 for an average room. Texture, height and stains push that up fast: popcorn is quoted at $2–$4 per square foot, vaulted ceilings at $2–$5, and decorative coffered ceilings at $3.50–$7. If you paint it yourself, the paint is the main cost, at $20–$70 a gallon in one guide, and a bedroom ceiling often needs only one or two gallons.

Per square foot, a flat ceiling is often no dearer than walls. The extra cost sits in the awkward cases, and this guide prices them, works through a paint example, and covers the prep that matters most: stains, texture and, in older homes, asbestos. For wall prices, see our guides to [painting a room](/blog/cost-to-paint-a-room) and [painting a bedroom](/blog/cost-to-paint-a-bedroom).

<Callout type="takeaway">
- **Pro, flat ceiling:** about $1.37–$3 per sq ft (Homewyse, May 2026, and a second 2026 guide), or $200–$500 for a typical room.
- **Harder ceilings:** popcorn $2–$4, vaulted or cathedral $2–$5, water-damaged $2.50–$4.50, coffered $3.50–$7 per sq ft.
- **DIY:** ceiling paint $20–$70 a gallon; about $40–$90 all in for a 12x12 ceiling if you also buy a roller, tape and a drop cloth.
- **Always:** ceiling paint is usually flat, goes on before the walls, and a leak must be fixed before any paint touches a stain.
- **Older textured ceiling:** do not sand or scrape until it has been tested for asbestos.
</Callout>

Cost guides price ceilings per square foot of ceiling, so the first job is to measure: length times width. The table below puts two sources side by side and adds our own arithmetic for a 12x14 ft room (168 sq ft). Fixr's figures were last updated in January 2025. The other guide (Facade Colorizer, published April 2026 and updated in November) comes from a paint-color tool site, so treat it as one data point, not a verdict.

A vaulted ceiling has more surface than the floor beneath it, so measure the slope itself or ask the painter how they measure (this is our inference from geometry, not a source claim).

### Cost by room

Fixr also lists whole-ceiling prices by room: a 50 sq ft bathroom at $50–$125 (painters often have a $150 or higher minimum), a 150 sq ft kitchen at $150–$375 and a 200 sq ft bedroom at $200–$500. On tiny ceilings the minimum charge, not the paint, sets the price.

## Why a ceiling can cost more per square foot than walls

Published numbers do not show a ceiling premium for a plain, flat ceiling. Bob Vila's cost guide (2021, modified 2022) gave $2–$6 per square foot for interior painting with labor and materials, averaging $3.30, against $1.37–$3 for a flat ceiling here. The sources are different years, so this is a rough comparison.

The premium shows up in specific conditions. Fixr says taller ceilings may need scaffolding or long-handled tools and that cathedral and vaulted ceilings can cost about 50% more than lower ones because they are slower and harder to paint. Fixr's per-height averages climb from about $1 per square foot at 8–9 ft to $1.50 at 10–12 ft and $2.50 at 12–18 ft. The 2026 Facade Colorizer guide puts scaffolding rental at $150–$300 per day for tall or two-story ceilings.

Our inference, not a source claim: overhead work is slower and messier, so painters price the hours and the risk, not just the area.

## What you pay to DIY

Ceiling paint is the main line item. Fixr puts it at $20–$70 per gallon, and a gallon is quoted at about 350–400 sq ft per coat. Bob Vila's guide (2022) puts general latex wall paint at $20–$100 per gallon, so ceiling paint tends to sit in the lower half of that range, though premium brands can overlap. Check current retailer prices, because sales move them.

| DIY item | Typical cost | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Ceiling paint | $20–$70 per gallon | Fixr, Jan 2025 |
| Roller covers and frame | $10–$100 for rollers in general | Bob Vila, 2022 |
| Angled brush | $3–$20 | Bob Vila, 2022 |
| Painter's tape | $3–$10 per roll | Bob Vila, 2022 |

Sherwin-Williams recommends an extension pole so you can roll the ceiling from the floor instead of a ladder, a low-nap roller cover for smooth ceilings and a 3/4-inch nap for textured ones. We did not find a price for the pole in a source we could read, so check the store. The same guide recommends tape along the wall line and a quality primer on water-stained ceilings. It rates the job as beginner level and about one day.

## How much paint do you need?

Sherwin-Williams' calculator lists about 350–400 sq ft per gallon of paint and 200–300 sq ft per gallon of primer. Bob Vila (2022) cites about 400 sq ft per gallon on smooth surfaces and about 250 on textured ones. Use 350 for smooth and 250 for textured if you want to avoid a second trip.

Worked example (our arithmetic) for a 12x14 ft bedroom:

1. Area is 12 x 14 = 168 sq ft.
2. Two coats means 336 sq ft of coverage.
3. Smooth ceiling: 336 / 400 = 0.84 gallons, or 336 / 350 = 0.96 gallons. One gallon is barely enough, so buy a gallon plus a quart for touch-ups, or two gallons if the ceiling is old and thirsty.
4. Textured ceiling: 336 / 250 = about 1.35 gallons, so buy two.
5. Paint cost at Fixr's $20–$70 per gallon: $20–$70 for one gallon, $40–$140 for two.

Against $168–$504 for a pro on the same ceiling, the DIY saving is mostly labor.

## Flat finish, and why

The paint makers and guides we read agree on flat. Benjamin Moore recommends an ultra-flat ceiling paint because the finish hides common ceiling imperfections and is formulated to minimize lap marks and splatter. Clare's guide says a flat white reflects light, downplays imperfections and has high hiding power. We did not find a paint maker recommending a glossier sheen for an ordinary ceiling; a shiny finish shows every ripple and patch under raking light, which is our inference.

## Painting over popcorn or texture

HomeLight puts painting over popcorn at about $1–$3 per square foot, against $2–$6 per square foot for professional removal and $1–$2 per square foot for DIY removal. Fixr gives a lower removal range of $1–$2 per square foot with a typical $900–$2,500 total. For our 168 sq ft example, painting over costs roughly $168–$504, while professional removal at HomeLight's rate would be $336–$1,008. Sources disagree, so get a local quote.

HomeLight adds that painted popcorn is harder to remove later, raising the removal cost by 50% or more. Clare and Sherwin-Williams both advise a 3/4-inch nap roller for texture, and Clare says to buy extra paint.

### Asbestos in older textured ceilings

The US Consumer Product Safety Commission says asbestos may be present in textured paint and in patching compounds used on wall and ceiling joints, and notes a 1977 ban on those products. It says you cannot tell by sight, to treat the material as asbestos-containing if in doubt or have it sampled by a qualified professional, and that taking samples yourself is not recommended. Its main advice is to leave material in good condition alone, because sanding, scraping or drilling can release fibers. The EPA's asbestos page likewise lists textured paint and patching compounds and says exposure generally occurs when the material is disturbed or damaged. HomeLight lists $5–$20 per square foot for professional abatement, so testing before any scraping is cheap insurance.

Sources differ on which years are at risk, so do not rely on a build date alone. This is general information, not legal or safety advice; rules vary by state and locality.

## Water stains, mold and prep

Stains are where a cheap job turns expensive. Every source we read says to fix the leak first: Bob Vila warns that painting over an active leak just hides a problem, and that fixing the cause can cost more than $3,000. The EPA's mold page says to fix leaks and dry everything, not to paint over mold because paint on a moldy surface is likely to peel, and that ceiling tiles can need replacing if they become moldy. It suggests you can usually handle about 10 square feet or less yourself and should hire an experienced contractor above that or after heavy water damage.

Once the ceiling is dry, Bob Vila recommends an oil-based, mold-resistant stain-blocking primer, because latex paint alone lets the stain bleed back. Restoration trade magazine R&R adds that shellac-based primers suit smoke and pet stains and that you should repaint the whole ceiling for a uniform look, not just the patch. Sagging or soft drywall, or a stain that keeps returning, is a job for a professional. Painters price this prep: see the water-damaged row in the table above.

## Ceiling first, then walls

Sherwin-Williams, Benjamin Moore, Clare and House Digest all say to paint the ceiling before the walls, so drips and spatter land on a surface you are about to paint anyway. Sherwin-Williams adds that if you will paint the walls afterward, you can skip taping the wall line and brush a few inches down the wall instead. The wait before starting the walls differs by source: House Digest says an hour is acceptable if the ceiling is dry to the touch, while Clare says 24 hours.

Fixr gives $300–$1,000 per room for walls and ceiling together, and our bedroom guide lists a typical professional add-on of $75–$250 for the ceiling alone, which is the number to compare against a separate quote. Doing both in one visit probably saves a second trip charge (our inference).

## DIY or hire a pro?

**Choose DIY if** the ceiling is flat, in good condition, under about 9 feet, and you can reach it with an extension pole. The paint is $20–$70, the job is rated beginner and about a day by Sherwin-Williams, and Clare says to let the ceiling dry at least 24 hours before moving on to the walls.

**Hire a pro if** the ceiling is high enough that you would need scaffolding ($150–$300 a day by itself), the texture is old and might contain asbestos, the stain is bigger than about 10 square feet or tied to mold, or the drywall is sagging. For a single flat room, a pro is mainly a time-saver; our guide to [rollers versus sprayers](/blog/paint-roller-vs-sprayer-cost) covers whether renting a sprayer for a textured ceiling makes sense.

## Common mistakes

- Painting over a stain before the leak is fixed and the ceiling has dried.
- Using plain latex instead of a stain-blocking primer on a water stain.
- Rolling a textured ceiling with a short nap, which leaves gaps.
- Overloading the roller, which Clare lists as a main cause of drips and spatter.
- Sanding or scraping old texture before it is tested.

## Ways to save

- **Do the prep yourself.** Clearing the room and covering floors is work most people can do before a painter arrives.
- **Book in the off-season.** The 2026 Facade Colorizer guide says painters have lighter workloads from November through February.
- **Combine jobs.** Ask for walls and ceiling in one quote.
- **Leave sound texture alone.** Painting over costs $1–$3 per square foot against $2–$6 to remove it, though painted texture is harder to remove later.
- **Spend on primer, not paint, if there is a stain.** A flat white at the low end of the $20–$70 range does the job on a sound ceiling.

## Renting? Check before you paint

A ceiling stain usually means a leak somewhere above, and the sources say the cause has to be fixed before anyone paints, so report it to your landlord instead of covering it. ## Ceiling painting cost FAQ

### How much does it cost to paint a ceiling?
A plain ceiling costs about $1.37–$3 per square foot for a professional, or roughly $200–$500 for a typical room, in two 2026 guides. Popcorn, vaulted and water-damaged ceilings run $2–$5 per square foot. Doing it yourself costs $20–$70 per gallon of paint, plus about $40–$90 for a basic 12x12 kit.

### Is it cheaper to paint a ceiling or walls?
Per square foot, a flat ceiling is usually the same or cheaper than walls: $1.37–$3 here against $2–$6 for interior painting in a 2022 guide. A room's ceiling is also smaller than its wall area. The exceptions are high, vaulted, textured and stained ceilings, where one guide says vaulted work can cost about 50% more.

### How much paint do I need for a ceiling?
Multiply length by width, double it for two coats and divide by coverage. Manufacturer and retailer guides quote 350–400 square feet per gallon on smooth surfaces and about 250 on textured ones. A 12x14 ceiling is 168 square feet, so one gallon covers a smooth one barely, and two gallons is safer for texture.

### Should ceiling paint be flat?
Yes in nearly every source we read. Benjamin Moore recommends an ultra-flat ceiling paint because it hides imperfections and minimizes lap marks and splatter, and Clare says flat finish downplays flaws. Pick a tinted ceiling color only if you want a feature ceiling; a flat white is the default.

### Do I have to prime a ceiling?
Not on a sound, previously painted ceiling, according to Benjamin Moore, but a primer is needed for new drywall, dark-to-light changes and stains from water, smoke or tannins. Use an oil-based or shellac-based stain blocker for stains, because ordinary latex lets them bleed through.

### Can I paint over a popcorn ceiling?
Yes, and it costs about $1–$3 per square foot for a pro according to HomeLight. Use a 3/4-inch nap roller and extra paint. If the home is old, have the texture tested before any sanding or scraping, and know that painted texture is harder to remove later.
