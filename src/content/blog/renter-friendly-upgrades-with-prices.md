Not every "renter-friendly" upgrade is actually risk-free — some just have a lower chance of a problem, not zero. Here are ten upgrades ranked by real cost and how likely each one is to cause a deposit issue.

| Upgrade | Typical cost | Deposit risk |
|---|---|---|
| Adhesive hooks/hangers | $15–$40 | Very low |
| Removable wallpaper (accent wall) | $70–$190 | Low |
| Peel-and-stick backsplash | $60–$150 | Low |
| Tension curtain rods | $15–$35/window | Very low |
| Furniture pads + rearranging | $10–$20 | None |
| LED under-cabinet lighting (stick-on) | $25–$60 | Very low |
| Freestanding shelving units | $40–$150 | None |
| Door/cabinet knob swaps (keep originals) | $20–$60 | Very low, if you keep the old hardware |
| Painting a full room | $50–$270 | Medium (repaint before move-out usually required) |
| Drilled shelving/anchors | $10–$60 | Medium–high (per hole charges add up) |

## Why the ranking isn't just about price

The cheapest option on this list — drilled shelving — is also one of the riskiest for your deposit. A $10 pack of anchors can turn into a $100+ deduction if you don't patch and repaint the holes properly. Compare that to removable wallpaper, which costs more upfront but has a real chance of getting your full deposit back with zero extra work at move-out.

## Bottom line

If you're not sure whether an upgrade is worth the risk, ask one question: "if this goes wrong, what does it cost to undo?" Adhesive and freestanding options almost always answer that question better than anything that requires a hole in the wall.
