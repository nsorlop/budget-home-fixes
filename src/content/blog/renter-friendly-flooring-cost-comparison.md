Bad flooring is one of the hardest things to ignore in a rental, and also one of the easiest to cover without a permanent change.

## Cost breakdown by option

For an average room (roughly 120 sq ft):

| Option | Typical cost | Install | Deposit risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Large area rug(s) | $80–$250 | None | None |
| Peel-and-stick vinyl tiles | $90–$220 | 2–4 hours, DIY | Low (residue possible on removal) |
| Interlocking foam/vinyl click tiles | $120–$300 | 1–3 hours, no adhesive | Very low |
| Floating laminate/vinyl plank (glued or nailed) | $350–$900 | 1–2 days, often needs a pro | High (semi-permanent) |

## Why peel-and-stick tiles beat a rug on hard floors

A rug covers cosmetic issues (stains, scratches) but doesn't fix a floor that's genuinely uneven, cold, or damaged. Peel-and-stick or click-together vinyl tiles create an actual new surface, which matters more in kitchens and bathrooms where rugs aren't practical anyway.

## Why click tiles beat peel-and-stick for full reversibility

Interlocking click tiles (no adhesive, they just connect to each other and lay on top of the existing floor) cost slightly more than peel-and-stick but leave zero adhesive residue risk — worth the extra $20–$50 for anyone particularly worried about their deposit.

## When to just use a rug

- The floor itself is in decent shape and you just want to soften/warm up the room.
- You move often — rugs are the fastest option to pack and reuse in the next place.

## When floating flooring is worth the deposit risk

- You've gotten explicit written approval from your landlord.
- You're planning to stay multiple years and the existing floor is genuinely unusable (not just outdated).

## Bottom line

For most renters, interlocking click-vinyl tiles are the sweet spot: real floor coverage, no adhesive risk, and full reversibility at move-out. Save rugs for rooms where the floor itself is fine, and skip glued/nailed flooring unless you have written landlord approval.
