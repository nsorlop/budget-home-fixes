For a single room, a roller is almost always the cheaper way to paint, and it is often not much slower once you count the prep. A rented airless sprayer costs about $85–$100 a day in the guides we read, uses roughly 25–33% more paint than rolling according to several sources, and needs everything you do not want painted covered first. By our own arithmetic, one bedroom comes to about $215–$255 sprayed versus about $100 rolled.

The picture shifts when the job is large, the surface is textured or fiddly, or the space is empty. Below are the sourced prices for renting and buying, what the sources say about paint waste, speed and cleanup, and two worked examples so you can find the break-even point for your own job. For what the paint itself costs, see our guides to [painting a bedroom](/blog/cost-to-paint-a-bedroom) and [painting a living room](/blog/cost-to-paint-a-living-room).

<Callout type="takeaway">
- **Rental:** about $85–$103 per day for an airless sprayer and $412–$500 per week in the two most specific guides, plus up to about $100 if you return it dirty. Other guides quote lower, so call your store.
- **Paint use:** most sources say 25–33% more paint than rolling; one says up to twice as much; one interior guide budgets only 5–10% extra.
- **Time:** spraying applies paint 2–3x faster in one vendor's figures, but masking can take at least three times as long as with a roller.
- **Our arithmetic:** one room, roller about $100 vs. rented sprayer about $215–$255. Whole apartment, roller about $345 vs. sprayer about $620–$710.
- **Rule of thumb:** roll one or two furnished rooms; consider spraying empty spaces, several rooms, textured ceilings or cabinets.
</Callout>

## What sprayers and rollers cost to buy

A roller setup is the baseline. Family Handyman puts a complete roller, pad and tray set under $30 (updated December 2023), and our [bedroom guide](/blog/cost-to-paint-a-bedroom) lists a roller and tray at $10–$25, plus a brush, tape and drop cloths. Sprayer prices depend on the type.

### The main sprayer types

| Type | Where it fits | What the sources say |
|---|---|---|
| Airless (1,500–3,600 psi per Consumer Reports) | Large surfaces, whole rooms | Fast, but more overspray. Graco rates its 3,000 psi X5 for interior projects and homeowners who paint about quarterly. |
| HVLP (high volume, low pressure) | Trim, doors, cabinets, furniture | Less waste and a finer finish; easier for a beginner to control. |
| Handheld cup gun | Small jobs, single pieces | HomeRight's $79.99 model has a 40-ounce cup and lists walls among its uses. Wagner's Control Spray Max page says it is not recommended for paint and primer. |

Sprayers are not interchangeable, and some are built for stain or thin coatings, so check the model is rated for your paint. Consumer Reports and Family Handyman both note that some paint has to be strained or thinned first.

## How much extra paint does spraying use?

Paint waste is the cost people miss, and the sources give a range, not one number.

| Source | Extra paint when spraying |
|---|---|
| Bob Vila (2021) | About 33% more than rolling |
| Rollingdog Tools (a tool vendor) | 25–33% more; rollers lose about 5–10% |
| Brad the Painter (2023) | 33% up to 100% more; airless transfer about 50%, HVLP up to about 90% |
| Family Handyman (Dec. 2023) | Add 5–10% for overspray when spraying interiors |

Sources differ, and none of them ran a test we could find. Brad the Painter blames overspray and high pressure blowing paint back into the air, and says worn tips make it worse and HVLP wastes less than airless.

Whole gallons widen the gap. A 12x12 bedroom needs about 1.9 gallons for two coats when rolled (the math is in our [bedroom guide](/blog/cost-to-paint-a-bedroom)). Add 25–33% and you need about 2.4–2.5 gallons, so you buy three. Even a 5–10% allowance takes it just past two. Seriously Painting's blog adds that filling and priming a 50-foot hose takes about a liter of paint.

## Speed, masking and cleanup

**Application speed.** One tool vendor puts sprayers at 400–500 sq ft per hour and rollers at 150–250. Treat that as a rough vendor figure, not a test. Consumer Reports notes that manufacturers claim up to ten times faster than a brush or roller, and says real savings depend on the job.

**Masking.** This is where the time goes. Consumer Reports says masking can take at least three times as long as for a brush and roller. Bob Vila says total project time ends up roughly equal, because the saved painting time is spent on masking and cleanup. Seriously Painting's blog gives a large-ceiling example of under four hours of masking against about 30 minutes of spraying for 1,500 sq ft. A masking machine, a hand tool that dispenses tape and paper, cuts masking time roughly in half, per Family Handyman. If you rent, do the masking before pickup so the daily rate pays for spraying, not taping.

**Cleanup.** Bob Vila says a filled sprayer has to keep working, because paint left in the hose can dry and clog it. The Olive & June blog says changing colors means flushing the whole system. A dirty return can cost up to about $100.

## Finish quality

Sprayers leave a smoother surface. Family Handyman says even a thin-nap roller leaves a lightly pebbled finish, while a sprayer gives a smooth, even coat. That matters most on cabinets, doors and trim. There are trade-offs. Sprayed paint needs a clean surface to stick (Bob Vila), and Honey Built Home says professionals often back-roll after spraying so the wall matches a roller texture, which makes later touch-ups blend in. On textured surfaces the sources split: Bob Vila and Olive & June favor sprayers, while Seriously Painting's blog says rollers suit them better and sprayers can miss spots.

## Worked cost examples (our arithmetic)

These are our own calculations from the sourced unit prices above, not quotes. Assumptions: paint at $35 per gallon (the middle of the $30–$40 mid-range tier in our bedroom guide), 350 sq ft per gallon per coat, two coats, a roller kit of up to $30, extra masking supplies of $20 for one room and $60 for the apartment (our assumption, no source), and 25–33% more paint when spraying. Rentals are one day at $85–$103 for the room and two days for the apartment. Speeds are the vendor figures above.

**One 12x12 bedroom (about 334 sq ft of wall)**

| | Roller | Rented airless sprayer |
|---|---|---|
| Paint | 2 gal = $70 | 3 gal = $105 |
| Tools or rental | up to $30 | $85–$103 |
| Consumables | included | $5–$25 |
| Extra masking | $0 | $20 |
| **Total** | **about $100** | **about $215–$255** |
| Painting time | 2.7–4.5 hrs | 1.3–1.7 hrs |

The sprayer saves about one to three hours of painting and costs about $115–$155 more. Add the extra masking time and most of the saving disappears, so for one room the roller wins.

**Whole apartment (about 1,500 sq ft of wall, roughly four to five bedroom-sized rooms)**

| | Roller | Rented airless sprayer |
|---|---|---|
| Paint | 9 gal = $315 | 11–12 gal = $385–$420 |
| Tools or rental | up to $30 | 2 days = $170–$206 |
| Consumables | included | $5–$25 |
| Extra masking | $0 | $60 |
| **Total** | **about $345** | **about $620–$710** |
| Painting time | 12–20 hrs | 6–7.5 hrs |

The sprayer costs about $275–$365 more and could save 4.5–14 hours of painting before masking. If masking eats some of that and you net eight hours, the extra cost works out to roughly $34–$46 per hour of your time. That can be a fair trade in an empty apartment if you dislike rolling, and a poor one in furnished rooms that need heavy masking. Buying a $399 sprayer instead only pays off if you have more painting ahead.

## Which jobs suit a sprayer and which suit a roller

| Job | Better choice | Why (per the sources) |
|---|---|---|
| One or two furnished rooms | Roller | In a lived-in home, masking can take longer than spraying saves (Paint Philly) |
| Whole apartment, empty | Sprayer, or hire a pro | Speed pays off on large flat areas with nothing to protect |
| Popcorn or textured ceilings | Sprayer | Bob Vila and Olive & June say a sprayer copes with texture (sources split, see above) |
| Kitchen cabinets, doors, trim | Sprayer, HVLP for detail | Smooth finish on small, intricate surfaces; see our [cabinet painting cost guide](/blog/cost-to-paint-kitchen-cabinets) |
| Touch-ups | Roller or brush | A roller matches an existing rolled texture |

Professionals mix methods. Paint Philly says painters choose by surface, project size, finish, time and whether the home is occupied, and use sprayers mainly in empty rooms, new construction, cabinets and exteriors. Our reading is that a sprayer is a productivity tool for crews with masking gear and practice, not an automatic upgrade for a first-timer. Consumer Reports calls sprayers better suited to contractors than most homeowners.

**Decision rule:** one or two furnished rooms, roll. Three or more empty rooms, textured ceilings, or cabinets, and time to mask properly: price a rental against a roller kit and against a professional quote.

## Safety and ventilation

<Callout type="warning" title="Spraying indoors needs more than an open window">
Family Handyman says indoor spray painting always calls for a respirator with organic-vapor cartridges, even with water-based paint, and that N95 dust masks do not stop VOCs. It suggests working in one room with the door closed, exhausting air out a window with a fan, and closing HVAC registers so fumes stay out of the ducts. Ohio State University Extension adds that paints and solvents are flammable, so keep heat and sparks away. Follow the product labels; this is general information, not safety advice for your paint.
</Callout>

Rolling needs none of this beyond normal ventilation.

## Renting an apartment? Check before you spray

Overspray settles on floors, windows, vents and fixtures, so a sprayer is a bigger risk to a deposit than a roller. Get written permission, mask everything, and read our guide to [getting your full security deposit back](/blog/get-your-full-security-deposit-back). For a single color change, a roller or [peel-and-stick wallpaper](/blog/peel-and-stick-wallpaper-vs-paint) is simpler.

## Paint sprayer vs. roller FAQ

### Is a paint sprayer worth it for interior painting?
Usually not for one furnished room: masking takes longer than rolling and the sprayer uses more paint. It is more likely to pay off in empty spaces, across several rooms, on textured ceilings, or for cabinets and trim where a smooth finish matters.

### How much does it cost to rent a paint sprayer?
Recent guides put airless sprayers at about $85–$103 a day and $412–$500 a week, though one 2026 guide lists $40–$110 a day. Add a possible deposit, a damage waiver, and up to about $100 for a dirty return.

### Do sprayers really use more paint?
Every source we read that gave numbers says yes. The size varies: 25–33% is the most common figure, one source says up to 100%, and Family Handyman budgets 5–10% for interiors. HVLP generally wastes less than airless.

### Should I buy or rent a sprayer?
Rent for a one-time job of a few days. Buy if you paint often: a week's rental is close to the $399 MSRP of Graco's Magnum X5, and Brad the Painter suggests buying if you regularly paint more than a couple of interior rooms.
