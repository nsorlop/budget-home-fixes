Paint sprayers look like the "pro" option, and rental counters make them sound cheap per day. Once you add masking time and paint waste, the comparison isn't as one-sided as it looks.

## Equipment cost

| | Roller setup | Sprayer (rented) |
|---|---|---|
| Equipment cost | $15–$30 (roller, tray, extension pole) | $40–$70/day rental |
| Owned equipment (one-time) | Same as above | $100–$300 for an entry-level airless sprayer |

## Paint usage

This is the number that surprises people: sprayers use 20–33% more paint than rollers for the same wall area due to overspray, even with careful technique. On a $40/gallon paint, that's an extra $15–$25 in paint alone for an average room.

| | Roller | Sprayer |
|---|---|---|
| Paint needed (average bedroom, 2 coats) | 2 gallons | 2.5–2.7 gallons |
| Masking/prep time | 20–30 minutes | 1–2 hours (everything not being painted must be fully covered) |

## Real total cost, one room

| | Roller | Sprayer (rented) |
|---|---|---|
| Equipment | $20 | $55 |
| Extra paint from overspray | $0 | $20 |
| **Total extra cost vs. paint itself** | **$20** | **$75** |

## When a sprayer is still worth it

- You're painting multiple rooms or a whole house in one project — the time savings per room adds up and starts to offset the extra paint cost.
- The surface is textured (stucco, popcorn ceilings, board-and-batten) where a roller struggles to get even coverage.
- You already own a sprayer from a previous project.

## When a roller wins

- A single room or a few rooms — the masking time for spraying isn't worth it at this scale.
- You're in a rented space and can't risk overspray on floors, trim, or fixtures you don't want painted.

## Bottom line

For one or two rooms, a roller is cheaper and lower-risk once you count paint waste and masking time. Sprayers only pull ahead on large, multi-room jobs or textured surfaces where rolling is genuinely impractical.
