A living room is usually the biggest room you paint, and the published prices show it. If you do the work yourself, the paint for a typical living room runs about $60 to $190, and a guide that adds tools and supplies lands around $90 to $180. If you hire a painter, 2026 guides quote anywhere from $300 to about $1,800 for the walls, and the sources disagree more here than for a bedroom.

The gap comes down to room size, ceiling height and how much is included in a quote. This guide sets the numbers side by side, shows which ones conflict, and walks through the choices that move the total most: the ceiling, color change, paint finish and how much paint to buy.

<Callout type="takeaway">
- **Pro, walls only:** $300–$700 for a 15×18 ft room in the lowest-priced guide we read, but $600–$1,800 for a room that size or larger with higher ceilings in two others. Get written quotes before trusting any average.
- **DIY:** about 3 gallons for a 15×18 room, so roughly $63–$189 in paint depending on brand. A guide that also counts supplies puts the total at $90–$160.
- **Ceiling:** about $100–$250 to have it painted. Vaulted or two-storey ceilings add $100–$300 in one guide, and another prices a vaulted ceiling alone at $300–$500.
- **Finish:** eggshell is the usual pick; satin costs about $3 more per gallon and wipes clean more easily in a busy room.
- **Color change:** going from dark to light can double the paint you buy, so price primer before you commit.
</Callout>

## Living room painting cost by size

Room dimensions drive both the paint and the labor. The pro and DIY columns below come from one 2026 cost guide (ThePaintCalculator) because it prices several living room sizes on the same basis. The wall area and gallons are our own calculation for 8 ft ceilings, two doors and three windows, using 350–400 sq ft of coverage per gallon per coat.

| Living room size | Wall area to paint (our calc.) | Paint for 2 coats | DIY cost (guide) | Professional, walls only (guide) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Small, 12×15 ft | about 347 sq ft | 2 gallons | $60–$120 | $250–$550 |
| Standard, 15×18 ft | about 443 sq ft | 3 gallons | $90–$160 | $300–$700 |
| Standard, 15×20 ft | about 475 sq ft | 3 gallons | $100–$180 | $350–$850 |
| Large, 18×24 ft | about 587 sq ft | 3–4 gallons | $150–$250 | $500–$1,200 |
| Open plan, 20×30 ft | about 715 sq ft | 4–5 gallons | $200–$380 | $700–$1,800 |

Other guides price living rooms higher. Unique Home Guide puts a room of 15×18 or larger with higher ceilings at $600–$1,800. Carolina Renew (updated August 2026) says $600–$1,500 for a standard living room and $1,000–$1,500 for a great room with tall ceilings. Fixr quotes $900–$1,600 for a 330 sq ft room, FacadeColorizer $900–$2,000 for about the same size, and Paint Medics $600–$2,000.

So the same 15×18 room is $300–$700 in one guide and $600–$1,800 in another. None of the pages explains the gap. Our guess is that the lower figures assume basic prep, walls only and mid-range work, while the higher ones fold in taller ceilings, trim or a painter's usual minimum. That is why a written quote beats any average.

## Why a living room costs more than a bedroom

For the same 8 ft ceilings, our arithmetic gives about 443 sq ft of paintable wall in a 15×18 living room against roughly 335 sq ft in a 12×12 bedroom, which is about a third more. Size is only part of it. Living rooms also tend to have:

- **More to cut around.** Windows, wide openings, built-in shelves, a mantel and crown molding all take slow, detailed brushwork. Paint Medics singles out vaulted ceilings and intricate trim as the reasons living rooms sit at the top of its per-room list.
- **Trim that adds up.** Carolina Renew says including trim and doors raises a job by roughly 30–40%.
- **Taller or sloped ceilings.** More on that below.
- **Furniture.** Fixr puts moving furniture at $50–$70 per room when a crew does it.
- **Repairs.** Fixr lists wall repairs at $75–$350 per room, and a room with a TV mount, gallery wall or shelving can have plenty of holes to fill.

For the bedroom equivalent of these numbers, see our guide to the [cost to paint a bedroom](/blog/cost-to-paint-a-bedroom).

Guides price professional labor at about $1.50–$3 per sq ft of wall (Unique Home Guide), $1.50–$3.50 (Fixr), $1.50–$4 depending on quality tier (Carolina Renew) or $2–$6 (Paint Medics).

Timing differs too. ThePaintCalculator says a crew finishes a 15×20 room's walls in 4 to 6 hours and a DIYer needs a full day. Unique Home Guide expects a first-timer to need a weekend, and Fixr gives two to four days once priming and prep are counted.

For a 16×14 room, our back-of-the-envelope estimate is $593–$1,185 for a pro at $1.50–$3 per sq ft against about $105 in paint at $35 a gallon for you. Choose DIY if:

- the ceilings are 8–9 ft and you can reach the top of the walls from a stepladder;
- the walls are in good shape and the color change is modest;
- you can spare a weekend and clear the room.

Hire out if the room has a vaulted ceiling, stairwell walls or heavy patching, or if you would rather pay for someone else's time.

## Ceilings, trim and other add-ons

Quotes for walls usually leave these out, so ask whether each one is included.

| Add-on | Professional cost | DIY materials |
|---|---|---|
| Ceiling (about 15×20 ft) | $100–$250 (ThePaintCalculator); $150–$250 (Carolina Renew) | $25–$50 |
| Trim and baseboards | $100–$300 | $20–$40 |
| High or vaulted ceilings | +$100–$300 (ThePaintCalculator); $300–$500 for a vaulted ceiling (Carolina Renew) | equipment rental, not priced in these guides |
| Furniture moving | $50–$70 per room (Fixr) | your time |
| Wall repairs | $75–$350 per room (Fixr) | spackle, sandpaper, a few dollars |

Fixr prices ceilings at $0.50–$3 per sq ft, and one painting company's page quotes $5–$9 per sq ft, several times higher than the others. The sources do not explain that, but it is a reminder that an individual contractor can price well above the aggregator averages.

### Open plans and tall rooms

Ceiling height is the biggest hidden multiplier. Improovy says higher ceilings raise the cost of a room 10–40%, and Unique Home Guide notes that anything beyond 8 ft often means scaffolding or extension equipment plus a labor surcharge. Simple geometry shows why: on our 15×18 example, a 10 ft ceiling instead of 8 ft lifts the wall area from about 443 to about 575 sq ft, a 30% jump, and a ladder alone stops being enough.

In an open plan, measure every connected wall and decide where the color stops before you buy paint. In a two-storey or vaulted room, the extra reach is the reason to hire; we would not stretch an extension ladder over a stairwell to save a few hundred dollars.

## Accent walls

An accent wall is the cheapest way to change a living room, but the quoted prices are all over the place. ThePaintCalculator says $50–$150 for a pro or $15–$30 in DIY paint. AccentWalls.com says $250–$400 as part of a larger job and $400–$750 standalone. A 2024 post from a painting company gives $150–$250 for one wall and adds that the minimum project charge is $400–$600.

The likely explanation, based on those last two sources, is minimum charges: a painter will not drive out for a $100 job. If you want one wall painted professionally, bundle it with something else. As a DIY task it needs about one gallon.

## Primer and color changes

Painting over a similar color is the cheapest case. Going from dark to light costs more. One paint-calculator site (roompaintcalc.com, August 2026) estimates a black-to-white repaint in a 12×12 room takes about four gallons total, against two for light gray to white. Its cost table, based on about $45 per gallon of paint and $28 of primer, runs from roughly $90 for a small color change to $163 for a big one.

Sherwin-Williams says primer coverage is about 200–300 sq ft per gallon, less than paint. Its blog and the paint-calculator site both point to gray-tinted primer for very dark walls, which can cut the number of finish coats. Deep reds, yellows and blues can still bleed through and may need a stain-blocking primer instead.

Primer itself runs about $10–$20 per gallon in one guide and $16–$70 in Fixr's range, so ask a painter whether it is in the quote.

## Paint finish for a busy living room

Finish is where the price gap is smallest and the daily-use gap is largest.

| Finish | Best for | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| Matte | Formal, low-traffic rooms (Echo House) | Hides flaws well, but is the hardest of the three to clean |
| Eggshell | The default for most living rooms | Hides roller marks better, but is harder to clean than satin |
| Satin | Busy family rooms, rooms next to a kitchen or dining area | Shows lap marks and wall flaws more |

Bob Vila reports satin costing about $3 more per gallon than eggshell ($30.98 versus $27.98 for one brand) and describes it as tougher and easier to wash. Z&Z Painting (a Chicago contractor, March 2026) says satin over eggshell adds $15–$20 in materials for a 300 sq ft living room. Its own gallon prices suggest a smaller gap, and at $3 a gallon across three gallons our arithmetic gives about $9. Echo House says finishes within one product line differ by $3–$8 per gallon.

Our decision rule: pick eggshell if the walls have some texture or patching and the room is calm. Pick satin if the room has kids, pets or heavy use and the walls are smooth. For ceilings, Echo House says flat is the only sensible sheen, and for trim it recommends semi-gloss.

### What a gallon costs

The Carolina Renew price index (August 15, 2026) lists Behr interior at $29.98–$62.98 per gallon, Glidden at $20.98–$37.98, Valspar at $17.98–$58.98, Benjamin Moore at $60.99–$108.99 and Sherwin-Williams at $83.49–$130.99. Echo House puts premium paint at $55–$80, below that Sherwin-Williams range, so the sources differ on where premium starts. The index also caught a 30% Sherwin-Williams sale from August 14 to 24, and waiting for a promotion can beat switching to a cheaper line.

## How much paint do you need?

Sherwin-Williams says a gallon covers about 350–400 sq ft, and recommends keeping a little extra because texture and application affect coverage. The method: total wall area is the room's perimeter times ceiling height; subtract about 20 sq ft per door and 15 sq ft per window; multiply by coats; divide by 350.

Our worked example: a 16×14 room with 8 ft ceilings has a 60 ft perimeter and 480 sq ft of gross wall. Subtracting two doors and three windows (85 sq ft) leaves about 395. Two coats is 790 sq ft, which is 2.3 gallons at 350 sq ft, so buy 3. Add the ceiling and you will want another gallon or two.

## Ways to pay less

- **Do the prep, hire the painting.** Clearing furniture, patching and taping are things most people can do, and prep is a large share of a painter's hours.
- **Skip the ceiling if it is clean.** That avoids the $100–$250 add-on.
- **Time your purchase.** Retail paint is discounted often enough that one sale can outweigh the price gap between two lines.
- **Try a feature wall first.** One wall and one gallon can change the room. If you rent, see [peel-and-stick wallpaper vs. paint](/blog/peel-and-stick-wallpaper-vs-paint).
- **Weigh tools for a big room.** See our [roller vs. sprayer cost](/blog/paint-roller-vs-sprayer-cost) comparison for open plans.

## Questions to ask a painter

1. Is the quote labor only, or does it include paint and primer, and which brand and finish?
2. Are the ceiling, trim, doors and closets included?
3. What is your surcharge for ceilings above 8 or 10 ft, and do you use scaffolding?
4. Do you move furniture and protect floors, and is that on the quote?
5. What is your minimum charge, and how many coats are included?

## Living room painting FAQ

### How much does it cost per square foot to paint a living room?
Labor runs about $1.50–$3 per sq ft of wall in some guides and $2–$6 in others. Fixr gives $1.50–$3.50 for walls and $3–$4 per sq ft for the whole room including ceiling and trim. Your area and the scope of work decide where in that spread you land.

### How many gallons of paint does a living room need?
Most living rooms need 2 to 4 gallons for two coats of walls. Our 16×14 example needs 3, and an open plan of 20×30 ft needs 4–5. Add a gallon or two if you paint the ceiling.

### Should I paint the ceiling too?
If the ceiling is clean and white, you can usually leave it. Fresh walls under a yellowed or stained ceiling look mismatched. A professional ceiling is about $100–$250 in the guides we read, more if it is vaulted.

### Do I need primer to paint over a dark color?
Usually yes. Sherwin-Williams recommends priming when covering dark paint with light, and gray-tinted primer can save finish coats. Budget extra paint or a primer gallon at roughly $10–$70 depending on type.
