Living rooms cost more to paint than bedrooms mostly because of size — they're typically 30–50% larger, and often have more corners, windows, and doorways that slow down the job.

## DIY cost breakdown

For an average living room (roughly 16 ft x 14 ft with 8 ft ceilings):

| Item | Cost range |
|---|---|
| Paint (2 coats, mid-range brand) | $90–$170 |
| Primer (if needed) | $25–$40 |
| Brushes, rollers, trays | $15–$30 |
| Painter's tape, drop cloths | $12–$25 |
| **Total DIY cost** | **$142–$265** |

Open-plan living rooms that connect to a dining area or hallway push these numbers up fast — measure the full connected wall run, not just the "room," before buying paint.

## Hiring a professional painter

Because living rooms usually have more trim, windows, and furniture to work around, labor costs run a bit higher per square foot than a bedroom: expect $400–$1,000 for an average living room, depending on ceiling height and how much furniture needs moving.

| Approach | Typical total cost | Time |
|---|---|---|
| DIY | $142–$265 | 1 full weekend |
| Professional | $400–$1,000 | 1–2 days |

## Extra costs people forget for living rooms

- **Furniture moving**: if you're hiring movers or a painting crew to shift heavy furniture, add $50–$150.
- **Ceiling paint**: living room ceilings are painted less often than walls but show wear near light fixtures — a fresh ceiling coat adds roughly $30–$60 in paint alone.
- **Accent walls**: a single darker accent wall (common in living rooms) usually needs an extra coat to cover evenly, add ~$25–$40 in paint for that wall.

## Bottom line

Budget close to $150–$270 for a DIY living room repaint with mid-range paint, roughly double what a similarly-sized bedroom costs due to square footage. If you're renting, weigh this against [removable wallpaper](/blog/peel-and-stick-wallpaper-vs-paint) if you don't want to repaint again before moving out.
