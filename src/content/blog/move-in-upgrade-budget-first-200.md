Move-in costs (deposit, first month's rent, movers) already eat most people's budget. If there's $200 left for upgrades, the order you spend it in matters more than the total.

## Priority order, ranked by impact per dollar

| Priority | Upgrade | Cost | Why it's ranked here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Adhesive hooks/hangers (entryway, closet, bathroom) | $25–$40 | Immediate daily-use improvement, zero risk |
| 2 | Tension curtain rods + curtains | $40–$80 | Privacy + light control, no drilling |
| 3 | Plug-in LED under-cabinet or accent lighting | $30–$60 | Big visual impact for the cost, fully removable |
| 4 | One removable wallpaper or peel-and-stick backsplash accent | $60–$150 | Save for last — biggest single expense on this list |

Total for all four: roughly $155–$330, so most people will do 1-3 fully and treat 4 as a "when I have more budget" item.

## What to skip in the first $200

- **Painting a full room**: at $100-$200+ per room, it eats the entire budget for one room's worth of change. Better to wait until you're not also buying hooks, rods, and basic furniture.
- **Furniture you're not sure about**: cheap furniture bought in a rush to fill space is a common regret purchase — better to live with a room half-furnished for a few weeks than buy the wrong thing twice.

## Why this order specifically

Every item in priority 1-3 is fully reversible with zero deposit risk, so they're safe to do immediately, even before you're sure how long you'll stay. Priority 4 is the first upgrade with even a small deposit consideration, so it's worth waiting until you've lived in the space a few weeks and know it's worth the investment.

## Bottom line

Spend on reversible, zero-risk upgrades first (hooks, rods, plug-in lighting) — they solve the most immediate daily friction. Save any wall-covering upgrade for after you've settled in, once you know the space and your plans for it better.
