Paint wins on price almost every time. A gallon covers an accent wall for somewhere between $18 and $96, while peel-and-stick rolls for the same wall typically land between $100 and $225 at mainstream prices, and more if you pick a designer print. Wallpaper starts to make sense when the deciding factor is not price but reversibility, or when you want a pattern that paint cannot give you.

This guide compares the two on material cost, installation, lifespan, the walls each one works on, and the risk to a rental deposit. Most price sources here are cost guides from 2025–2026, and many of the wallpaper figures come from companies that sell wallpaper, so we point out where they disagree and which numbers are our own arithmetic.

<Callout type="takeaway">
- **Accent wall (about 96 sq ft):** one gallon of paint is roughly $18–$96; 4–5 budget peel-and-stick rolls are roughly $100–$225 (our estimate from published per-roll prices).
- **Pattern repeat can double your roll count.** Area math says 4 rolls for a 12×8 ft wall; a large straight-match pattern can push that to 8.
- **Peel-and-stick needs a smooth, cured, satin-type painted wall.** Manufacturers say 4 weeks after painting, or 30 days, before hanging it.
- **Expect about 3–5 years** from peel-and-stick, and about 4–7 years from a paint job, according to the sources we could compare.
- **Renters:** the wall finish decides the deposit risk more than the product does.
</Callout>

## What each option costs for the same wall

Peel-and-stick is sold in rolls of about 28 square feet (a 20.5-inch by 16.5-foot single roll), or double rolls of about 56 to 60 square feet. That is why most price guides quote per roll, and why it helps to convert to square feet before comparing.

Published per-roll prices for peel-and-stick vary:

- Taskrabbit's cost guide (updated September 2026) lists peel-and-stick at around $25 a roll.
- Fixr lists $20–$45 a roll (data from January 2025).
- Roman Products, an adhesives manufacturer, uses $35 per single roll, about $1.14 per square foot by its own math.
- Tempaper's double-roll listing runs from $12.99 (clearance) to $150 (designer collaborations) for 56 square feet.
- Giffywalls, a custom-print seller, charges $4.99 per square foot for peel-and-stick (July 2026), which is about $140 for the same 28 square feet.

So the mainstream range is roughly $0.90–$1.60 per square foot, and custom or designer prints run several times that. The sources do not explain the spread beyond design and brand, but the gap is consistent with off-the-shelf rolls versus custom-cut and licensed prints (our inference).

Paint is cheaper per square foot once you count that a gallon covers about 350–400 sq ft (Sherwin-Williams). An August 2026 price index lists gallons from $17.98 (Valspar ExpressCoat) and $20.98 (Glidden Essentials) up to $29.98 (Behr Premium Plus) and $95.99 (Sherwin-Williams Duration). Our full cost guides cover rooms and labor: see [bedroom painting costs](/blog/cost-to-paint-a-bedroom), [living room costs](/blog/cost-to-paint-a-living-room) and [bathroom costs](/blog/cost-to-paint-a-bathroom).

Sources disagree most on whole rooms. Anewall, a wallpaper seller, lists $800–$1,200 as the wallpaper cost for a 12×18 ft room and $320–$500 for paint. Fixr gives the same $800–$1,200 for that room but as the installed total, with materials at $350–$500 and labor at $450–$700. ## Whole room: where the gap widens

Take a 12×12 ft bedroom with 8-foot ceilings, one door and two windows. Walls come to about 334 sq ft (the same measurement used in our paint guide). Our estimates:

| Option | Whole room (334 sq ft) |
|---|---|
| Paint, 2 gallons, DIY | about $36–$192 for paint alone |
| Peel-and-stick, mainstream rolls | about $300–$540 for 12 rolls, which is a floor |
| Peel-and-stick, designer double rolls | up to about $900 (6 double rolls at $150) |
| Peel-and-stick, custom-cut | about $1,670 at $4.99 per sq ft |
| Professional wallpaper labor | about $670–$2,000 at $2–$6 per sq ft |

The twelve-roll figure assumes no pattern repeat and no mistakes. Our [removable wallpaper cost per room](/blog/removable-wallpaper-cost-per-room) guide goes room by room. For a full room, paint is several times cheaper in materials on our numbers. That is why wallpaper usually makes sense on one wall.

## Installation: difficulty, time and hiring help

Peel-and-stick is designed for DIY. Manufacturer guides call for a level, craft knife, smoother, tape measure and step stool. Panels can be lifted and repositioned during application, which forgives small errors. Katie Kime, a wallpaper brand, says most accent walls take under two hours. Giffywalls says one to three hours for an accent wall against one to two days for paint, once prep and drying between coats are counted. Drew & Jonathan, a home-improvement brand, says most rooms take an afternoon if you have proper tools and a helper.

If you hire out, Taskrabbit's guide puts labor at $56–$85 an hour (national average $62), $2–$6 per square foot, and minimum fees of $150–$300. It estimates 2–4 hours for an accent wall, 4–8 for a bathroom and 6–12 for a bedroom. Fixr adds $30–$70 per roll as another way installers price it. Do not assume peel-and-stick is cheaper to install professionally; none of the pages we read quoted a lower labor rate for it.

## Which walls work

This is where peel-and-stick fails most often. Every manufacturer page we read describes the same ideal wall: smooth, clean, dry, primed and painted in eggshell, satin or semi-gloss.

- **Textured walls:** Tempaper's install guide says avoid textured surfaces, while its FAQ tolerates orange-peel texture and no heavier. York says it does not work well on heavily textured walls. Treat any texture beyond light orange peel as a no.
- **Flat or matte paint:** Tempaper warns against it and says removal may damage the paint. Drew & Jonathan adds scrubbable and non-stick paints to the avoid list.
- **Glossy paint:** the sources split. Wallpaper From the 70s says glossy surfaces work if cleaned well. Giffywalls says gloss and heavy texture weaken the grip.
- **New paint:** wait. York says at least 4 weeks. Tempaper's FAQ and Wallpaper From the 70s say 30 days. Drew & Jonathan says allow at least 2 weeks when you repaint a wall light before papering over a dark one. If your plan was paint now, wallpaper next week, it does not work.
- **Bathrooms and kitchens:** York warns of bubbling and seam separation. Giffywalls says steam can lift edges over time. Tempaper says its product works with indirect moisture and steam but not sustained direct water. Keep it out of shower splash zones either way.

Traditional pasted wallpaper is more forgiving of slightly imperfect walls, according to Zeeko Home. It is also harder to remove. Katie Kime says it can last 15–20 years against 3–5 for peel-and-stick, needs steaming or stripping to come off, and a pro is recommended beyond a single accent wall. Fixr's removal figure is $0.75–$2 per square foot plus $50–$75 disposal. For a rental, that difference matters most: peel-and-stick is the version designed to come off.

## How many rolls to buy

The simple method, from WallPops: multiply wall height by width, divide by the roll's square footage (28 for a 16-foot roll, 30.75 for 18 feet) and round up. Buy one extra roll if you have a large repeat or high ceilings. That works for random-match patterns, which WallPops says waste nothing.

Straight-match and drop-match patterns are different, because each strip has to start at the right point in the design. WallPops says repeats can be 0–27 inches and that repeats over 21 inches need extra material. Here is a worked example. The numbers are ours.

**Wall: 12 ft wide by 8 ft high (96 sq ft), 20.5 in by 16.5 ft rolls (198 inches long).**

1. Strips across: 144 in ÷ 20.5 in = 7.02, so you need 8 strips. Seven strips would leave the wall half an inch short, so overlap and trimming push you to 8.
2. Area method: 96 ÷ 28 = 3.4, so 4 rolls.
3. Strip check with no repeat: two 96-inch strips fit in one 198-inch roll, but only if you trim within 6 inches total. 8 strips ÷ 2 = 4 rolls, with zero room for error.
4. Strip check with a 12-inch straight-match repeat: each strip must be about 108 inches (96 in plus trim, rounded up to the next repeat). Only one fits per 198-inch roll, so you need 8 rolls.

At $25–$45 a roll, that is $100–$180 for 4 rolls and $200–$360 for 8. Same wall, same brand, and the pattern doubled the bill. Check the repeat length on the product page before you fall for a design.

<Callout type="warning" title="Buy every roll at once">
Colors can differ between print runs, and York says to confirm that all rolls share the same lot number before you cut. A later reorder may not match, and coming up one strip short is worse than paying for an extra roll.
</Callout>

## Durability and lifespan

Sources differ, and most are sellers. Giffywalls says about five years before a refresh. Katie Kime says three to five years for peel-and-stick in ideal conditions and 15–20 for traditional. Anewall says wallpaper lasts 10–15 years against 3–5 for paint, but its page does not separate peel-and-stick from traditional paper, so we would not apply that to peel-and-stick. On the paint side, Paint Medics suggests repainting every 4–7 years.

Our reading: budget about 3–5 years for peel-and-stick and 4–7 for paint. Paint is easier to touch up; peel-and-stick can be washed with a damp cloth and mild soap (York).

## Renters: deposit risk and permission

The sources agree on how to take it down: start at a top corner, pull slowly and parallel to the wall, and use a hair dryer on stubborn spots. They also agree that clean removal depends on the wall. Tempaper warns of paint damage on flat or matte finishes. Drew & Jonathan recommends eggshell, satin or semi-gloss paint for easier removal later.

Paint carries a different risk: many leases require you to return the wall to its original color. Our [deposit guide](/blog/get-your-full-security-deposit-back) covers deductions, and our bedroom paint guide shows repainting costs $50–$160 in paint for a DIY job and $300–$900 for a pro. We could not load a landlord-side source, so this part is our suggestion: get permission in writing, and test a small panel in a low-visible spot for a week or two before committing to the whole wall.

## When paint wins, and when wallpaper wins

**Choose paint if:**

- You own the place or plan to stay more than a few years.
- The walls are textured, matte, unpainted or in poor shape.
- The room is a bathroom or kitchen with steam or splashes.
- You are covering a whole room on a small budget.

**Choose peel-and-stick if:**

- You rent, the wall is smooth satin or eggshell paint, and the lease or landlord allows it.
- One accent wall is the goal, and the pattern is the point (a color alone is cheaper in paint).
- You may move within a few years and want an easy exit.
- You can wait out the paint cure time, or the wall is already cured.

## Common mistakes

- **Skipping the pattern repeat** when calculating quantity.
- **Buying rolls one at a time** and getting different print runs.
- **Hanging over fresh paint.** Wait the full 4 weeks or 30 days.
- **Trusting corners or the ceiling as a guide.** Use a level.
- **Not cleaning the wall.** Tempaper suggests a 1:1 isopropyl alcohol and water wipe.
- **Peeling the whole backing at once.** Peel it in stages and swap dull blades often.

## FAQ: peel-and-stick wallpaper vs. paint

### Is peel-and-stick wallpaper cheaper than paint?

Not usually. Our estimate for a 96 sq ft accent wall is $18–$96 for a gallon of paint against about $100–$225 for mainstream peel-and-stick rolls. Paint stays cheaper for a full room. Wallpaper's case is reversibility, not price.

### Will peel-and-stick wallpaper damage my walls?

On smooth, cured satin or eggshell paint, manufacturers say it removes cleanly. Flat, matte, textured, freshly painted or non-stick paint raises the chance of lifting paint. Every source we read is a seller or publisher, so test a small area first.

### Can I use peel-and-stick wallpaper on textured walls?

Mostly no. York and Wallpaper From the 70s advise against textured surfaces, and Tempaper tolerates only light orange peel. If your wall is heavily textured, paint is the safer route. If it is only lightly textured, tape up a sample panel before you buy the whole order.

### How long after painting can I hang peel-and-stick wallpaper?

York says at least 4 weeks and Tempaper says 30 days. Drew & Jonathan allows 2 weeks after a light repaint. Tempaper's install guide defers to your paint maker's cure instructions, so check the can. Longer is safer, since paint keeps releasing gases while it cures.

### Can peel-and-stick wallpaper go in a bathroom?

Sources split. Tempaper says it handles indirect steam but not sustained direct water, while York warns of bubbling and separating seams in bathrooms and kitchens. For steamy rooms, paint is safer; see our [bathroom painting costs](/blog/cost-to-paint-a-bathroom).

### Should I hire someone to hang it?

For a single accent wall, sources treat DIY as realistic. A pro runs about $2–$6 per square foot with a $150–$300 minimum, which can pay off with large repeats, tall walls or expensive designer rolls, where one bad cut costs more than the labor.
