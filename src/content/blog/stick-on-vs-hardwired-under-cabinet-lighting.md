Under-cabinet lighting is one of the highest-impact-per-dollar kitchen upgrades, but the "which kind" question matters a lot if you're renting or want to keep costs down.

## Cost breakdown

For an average kitchen (covering 10–15 ft of counter run):

| | Stick-on/battery LED strips | Plug-in LED strips | Hardwired LED (electrician-installed) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Material cost | $25–$60 | $30–$80 | $80–$200 (fixtures + wiring) |
| Install labor | None (DIY, adhesive) | None (DIY) | $150–$400 (licensed electrician) |
| **Total** | **$25–$60** | **$30–$80** | **$230–$600** |

## What you give up at the cheaper end

- **Battery-powered strips**: no wiring at all, but batteries need replacing every few months with daily use, and light output is noticeably dimmer than wired options.
- **Plug-in strips**: brighter and no batteries, but you'll see a visible cord running to the nearest outlet unless your cabinets happen to line up with one.
- **Hardwired**: fully integrated, no visible cords, dimmable in most cases, but requires an electrician and isn't something you'd install in most rental units without approval.

## Deposit and reversibility

Stick-on and plug-in options are fully removable with no wall or cabinet modification — safe for any rental. Hardwired lighting is a permanent electrical modification and needs landlord sign-off; undoing it before move-out adds its own cost if required.

## Bottom line

For renters, plug-in LED strips are the best balance: noticeably brighter than battery strips, no ongoing battery cost, and still fully removable. Save hardwired lighting for a home you own or a rental where you've gotten explicit, written approval for electrical work.
