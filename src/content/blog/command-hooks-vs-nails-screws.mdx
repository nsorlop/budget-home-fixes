Adhesive hooks cost little more than nails once you price each wall point: a picture-hanging strip works out to about $0.50 a pair and a small utility hook to about $1.50, while a picture nail is under $0.50 and a hanger under $2. The real differences are weight limits, which surfaces the adhesive works on, and what you leave behind at move-out. Most reported damage comes from ignoring a rule the manufacturer prints on the package.

This guide uses 3M's own documents where we could reach them, September 2026 retail listings for prices, and two deposit guides for the nail-hole side. Where sources disagree we show both numbers, and anything marked as ours is our own arithmetic.

<Callout type="takeaway">
- **Ratings are small and specific.** 3M's picture-strip chart tops out at 16 lb for four pairs of large strips on a frame up to 24×36 in. Single hooks are rated 1, 3, 5 and 7.5 lb depending on the model.
- **Cost per point:** about $0.50 per strip pair and $1.50 per small or medium hook, versus $0.46 for a picture nail and $0.36 to $2.50 for an anchor (our division of pack prices).
- **Small nail holes are usually not chargeable,** per the two deposit guides we read, so nails only cost you at move-out if a landlord treats the holes as damage ($5 to $75 per hole in the two guides).
- **Never use adhesive for shelves,** items over a bed, valuables, wallpaper, brick or heavily textured walls.
</Callout>

## What 3M says the strips can hold

3M rates picture strips by number of pairs and by frame size, not weight alone. This chart comes from an undated 3M picture-hanging graphic with US sizes.

| Strip | 2 pairs | Max frame | 4 pairs | Max frame |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Small | 2 lb | 5×7 in | 4 lb | 8×10 in |
| Medium | 6 lb | 11×17 in | 12 lb | 18×24 in |
| Large | 8 lb | 16×20 in | 16 lb | 24×36 in |
| Narrow | 6 lb | 11×17 in | 12 lb | 18×24 in |

A 3M sheet for the UK and Europe lists the same tiers in kilograms: 5.4 kg for four medium pairs and 7.2 kg for four large pairs, roughly 12 and 16 lb (our conversion).

The sources do not fully agree. The Target listing for a medium-and-large pack labels the strips 10 lb and 15 lb, not 12 and 16, and the sources do not explain the gap. Our inference is that packaging changed between product generations. Use the lowest number you can find, and the one on your own pack. Zumper's renter guide says these strips hold "up to 8 pounds," a blanket figure that skips the pair-count and frame-size limits.

## Do not use adhesive hooks for these

<Callout type="warning" title="Skip adhesive strips and hooks for">
- **Shelves.** 3M's UK and Europe sheet says picture strips are not recommended for mounting shelves, tapestries or shadow boxes.
- **Anything over a bed.** 3M's usage tips and the Target listings say not to hang items over beds.
- **Valuable or irreplaceable items.** 3M's 2011 sheet names antiques and heirlooms.
- **Wallpaper.** 3M's instructions say not to use them on it.
- **Textured walls, brick, rough or non-stick surfaces, fabric.** Target's listing quotes 3M on all of these.
- **Freshly painted walls.** 3M says wait at least 7 days.
- **Dirty, dusty or oily surfaces,** bare wood or porous unpainted surfaces, and possibly vinyl, which 3M says may not hold well.
</Callout>

Two more rules from 3M's documents: use one hook per hung item, and do not stick strips to paper or fabric surfaces on a frame. Current retail listings say to install between 50 and 105°F; the 2011 sheet for water-resistant strips gives a wider range. Different product lines, different limits.

## Bathrooms and humidity

3M's UK and Europe chart marks picture-hanging, decorative, clear and general-purpose strips as indoor use excluding bathrooms or humid environments. The bath line has water-resistant strips, and the same document says it can go in a shower.

## What actually goes wrong

- **Wrong removal.** Paint Strategies says damage is usually tied to wiggling or yanking. 3M says never pull the strip toward you: pull the tab slowly straight down along the wall, at least 12 inches.
- **Paint not ready.** 3M says 7 days after painting. House Digest says latex takes around 30 days to fully cure and that Command's Australian site advises 28. If you painted within the last month, test a hidden spot first (our suggestion).
- **Skipping the wipe.** 3M says use isopropyl alcohol, not household cleaners, and that dirty or oily surfaces will not hold.
- **Loading too soon.** 3M says capacity drops if weight goes on before an hour has passed, and to press the back plate for 30 seconds.
- **Too heavy or too big.** House Digest and the retail listings put a weight limit on every pack; exceeding it can pull paint off with the strip.
- **Heat.** The 2011 sheet says the adhesive can soften above 125°F.
- **Leaving hooks up for years.** Family Handyman recommends taking them down every six months. The 3M documents we could read give no time limit, so that is one publication's advice.

If paint does lift, Paint Strategies says the spot needs repainting and priming. Family Handyman suggests warming a stubborn strip with a hair dryer for 20 to 30 seconds and working dental floss behind it.

## What a nail hole costs at move-out

Two deposit guides give overlapping but different pictures. Deposit Deadline (updated March 2026) says small picture-nail holes are normal wear and tear in most states. It sorts sizes: under about 1/4 in is normal wear, 1/4 to 1/2 in is a gray area, and 1/2 to 1 in is likely chargeable. Two to four holes per wall is clearly normal, five to ten small ones usually is, and dozens may be excessive. iPropertyManagement (August 2023) also treats small holes as normal wear but gives a flat charge of $5 to $25 per hole when they count as damage.

For the do-it-yourself fix, Deposit Deadline puts spackle and a putty knife at under $10 and patch kits at $5 to $10, against $25 to $75 per hole for a professional repair of larger holes. The sources do not explain why the per-hole figures differ; our inference is that one is a flat fee and the other a contractor repair. An 8 oz tub of DAP spackle was $8.59 at Ace.

This is general information, not legal advice. Deposit rules vary by state and leases add their own, so ask before drilling. Our [security deposit guide](/blog/get-your-full-security-deposit-back) covers deductions in more detail.

## A worked example: ten light frames

This is our own calculation from the prices above.

- **Adhesive:** two pairs of strips per frame is 20 pairs, about $10 at $0.50 a pair.
- **Nails:** one 10-pack at $4.59, plus a tub of spackle at $8.59 if you patch, is about $13.
- **Worst case for nails:** a landlord bills each hole at $5 to $25, or $50 to $250 for ten. The guides we read say small holes normally are not chargeable.

For light frames the upfront cost is a wash. Risk and rules decide it: adhesive risks a paint peel that no source puts a price on, and nails risk a lease objection. ## Alternatives that need no holes or adhesive

- **Over-door hooks.** A six-hook Target model was $15, listed at 20 lb, for doors up to 1.75 in thick. See our [over-door organizers vs. closet systems](/blog/over-door-organizers-vs-closet-systems) guide.
- **Tension rods.** A 28 to 48 in Target rod was $2.97 (clearance) and holds up to 5 lb; reviewers say it sags fully extended.
- **Freestanding shelving** for anything heavy: see [freestanding vs. built-in shelving cost](/blog/freestanding-vs-built-in-shelving-cost).
- **Putty, painter's tape or hook-and-loop** for posters and very light items, per Zumper.
- **Leaning a frame or mirror** on the floor or a ledge needs no fastener (our suggestion).

## FAQ

### Do Command strips damage walls?
Usually not when used as directed, but they can. The sources tie damage to pulling strips off the wrong way, freshly painted or textured walls, and overloading. 3M says pull the tab slowly straight down and wait 7 days after painting.

### How long can they stay up?
None of the 3M documents we could read state a limit. Family Handyman recommends removing them every six months, which is its own advice.

### Can I use them on textured walls or brick?
Target's listing quotes 3M as saying not to use them on textured walls or brick. Use hardware, freestanding storage or an over-door hook.

### Are Command strips safe for a mirror?
Only within the rating, frame size and pair count on the pack. 3M's chart tops out at 16 lb and a 24×36 in frame for four large pairs. Do not hang a mirror over a bed, and use anchors for a heavy or valuable one.

### Will a landlord deduct for nail holes?
Both guides say small picture-nail holes are normal wear and tear, while clusters, large holes and paint damage can be charged. State rules differ, so check yours.

### What if a strip will not come off?
Warm it with a hair dryer for 20 to 30 seconds and slide dental floss behind it. Do not scrape. If paint lifts, repaint and prime.
