Painting trim and baseboards is cheap in materials and slow in labor, which is why painters price it separately from walls. The published numbers we found put professional work at roughly $1 to $4.88 per linear foot for baseboards and casing, and roughly $70 to $200 for a standard interior door. A DIY job for one room needs only a quart or so of paint, so the real cost is the small kit and your time.

This guide compares those figures, works through a 12×12 room so you can see what the rates mean in dollars, and covers the order of work, what to prep, and when painting is not worth doing at all.

<Callout type="takeaway">
- **Hire a pro:** about $1 to $4.88 per linear foot of baseboard or casing depending on the source, and about $70 to $200 per interior door (one source calls $150 the average).
- **DIY:** a quart of trim paint is often enough for a room. Our estimate for a first-time kit (caulk, brush, tape, filler, sanding sponge, paint) is $45 to $105.
- **Time:** one room of baseboards is a typical room under a day, according to Bob Vila, and at least two days if you are new to painting or the boards need significant prep.
- **Skip it** when the trim is only dusty or lightly scuffed: clean first, and mask it if you are only repainting the walls.
</Callout>

## What painters charge for trim

Cost guides price trim two ways: per linear foot (baseboards, casing, trim) and per door. They do not agree, and several are marketing pages from painting companies, so treat the spread as the answer rather than any single number.

| Item | Published range | Source and date |
|---|---|---|
| Baseboards, whole job | $2.30 to $4.88 per linear foot | Homewyse, May 2026 |
| Interior trim and linear trim | $1.00 to $3.50 per linear foot | CountBricks, February 2026 |
| Door casing | $1 to $4 per linear foot | Fixr (January 2025) |
| Interior door, one painter's quote | $75 to $150 per door | CountBricks, February 2026 |
| Interior door, national average | $150 (range $70 to $200) | Fixr (January 2025) |

The Homewyse figure is the most transparent about what it covers. Its estimate assumes caulking, spot priming, two coats of acrylic paint-and-primer in eggshell or semi-gloss, up to about 2 hours of prep per 100 linear feet, and a 2-hour minimum labor charge. A painter's quote that is per linear foot for casing and per door for the slab will land near the middle of these ranges for a standard door.

The CountBricks page also shares a case study with a Houston painter: about $1,150 for five interior doors and more than 300 linear feet of trim, finished in two days. That is a single marketing example, not a benchmark. Our arithmetic: if the five doors cost $75 to $150 each ($375 to $750), the trim works out to roughly $1.30 to $2.60 per linear foot, which sits inside the ranges above.

## A worked example: one 12×12 bedroom

Here is how to turn the per-foot rates into a number for your own room. This is our arithmetic, not a quote.

1. **Measure the perimeter.** A 12×12 room has 48 feet of wall. Subtract about 3 feet for a door opening and you have roughly 45 linear feet of baseboard.
2. **Apply the rate.** At $1.00 to $3.50 per foot that is about $45 to $158. At $2.30 to $4.88 it is about $104 to $220.
3. **Add the door.** One interior door is $70 to $200, and if the frame and casing are billed separately, add the linear-foot rate for the casing. A 32×80 inch door has about 16 feet of casing per side, so about 32 feet if you paint both sides of the opening.
4. **Check minimums.** The Homewyse assumptions include a 2-hour minimum labor charge, so a very small job may cost more per foot than the table suggests.

Add the pieces up and one bedroom's baseboards plus a door and casing land at roughly $150 to $550 depending on the rate. That is why our [cost to paint a bedroom](/blog/cost-to-paint-a-bedroom) guide treats trim as an add-on and not part of the wall price. For the full room picture, see [how much it costs to paint a room](/blog/cost-to-paint-a-room).

## DIY materials: what you actually buy

Trim is small, so the paint barely matters. Fixr says a quart of interior paint (about $8 to $18) covers both sides of a 32×80 inch door for one coat. Our estimate is that 45 feet of 5 1/4 inch baseboard is only about 20 square feet, so a quart should cover two coats with some left over. Buy a quart, not a gallon, unless you are doing the whole house.

### Which paint

Bob Vila's best-paint-for-trim page recommends oil-based paint for trim because it fills small scratches and holds up to wear, with semi-gloss as the standard sheen and flat or matte discouraged since they are harder to wipe clean. Its how-to-paint-baseboards guide, by contrast, recommends semi-gloss latex, which is water-based. For most renters and first-timers, a water-based semi-gloss is the lower-hassle option (our judgment). Use a dedicated primer rather than a paint-and-primer when painting bare trim, switching from dark to light, or covering flaking paint.

## Order of work: trim or walls first

Sources disagree, and the honest answer is that either order works.

- **Trim first.** Hunker says cutting the wall paint in against finished trim is cleaner than cutting trim next to a wet wall, and taping trim is quicker and safer than taping walls. The cost is that roller spatter can land on finished trim.
- **Walls first.** Hunker lists this as the better choice if you are unsure of the wall color or have a less experienced helper, since drips hit surfaces you have not painted yet. Bob Vila's baseboard guide protects the floor and wall edges with tape or a paint guard.
- **Lighter color first, glossier last.** Hunker also suggests painting the lighter color first and the glossy surface last because gloss mistakes are harder to hide.

A sequence that fits all of this (our synthesis): patch and sand, caulk, prime where needed, paint the trim, then walls, then a final touch-up on the trim. Ask the Builder adds that the final trim coat is best applied after the walls are dry, carried to the wall line, and says to caulk the gaps after the woodwork is installed and to wipe the bead with a damp sponge. If you are only repainting walls and the trim is in good shape, tape it and leave it. If a ceiling is part of the job, our [ceiling painting cost guide](/blog/cost-to-paint-a-ceiling) covers it.

## Prep that decides how it looks

Bob Vila's baseboard walk-through is a good checklist: clean the boards, set any raised nail heads, fill holes and dents and sand them smooth, replace cracked caulk between baseboard and wall, wipe off the dust, then prime if the boards are new, stained or much darker. Paint two coats with the short edge of an angled brush, lightly sand between coats, and pull tape at a low angle while the paint is set but not fully cured. Ask the Builder warns that filler left unprimed can show as flat dots against glossy paint, so seal it, and that caulk over finished paint can look like pinstriping unless you paint over it.

Hunker cautions that painter's tape does not always peel off cleanly and can damage drywall, so press it down and remove it carefully. Bob Vila advises against pulling baseboards off just to paint them, because removing caulk often damages the wall.

## DIY or hire out

**Choose DIY if** you have a few rooms of baseboard, steady hands, and a weekend. Material cost is small, and prep is the part professionals charge for.

**Hire a painter if** you have lots of paneled or louvered doors, stained or varnished wood that needs sanding or priming, or high crown molding. Fixr lists 3 to 5 hours per door, which adds up quickly across a house.

**Do a hybrid** by caulking, filling and sanding yourself and paying for the painting. CountBricks lists doing minor prep yourself as a way to cut cost. Ask whether quotes include caulk, primer and two coats, since the Homewyse range assumes they do.

## Common mistakes

- **Skipping the clean.** Dust and grime get sealed under caulk and paint.
- **Masking tape instead of painter's tape,** which Ask the Builder warns against.
- **Not priming filler or bare wood,** which shows up as dull spots.
- **Painting over cracked caulk.** Replace it first.
- **Painting the door but not the frame.** The result looks mismatched, and frames are often priced per foot.
- **Using flat wall paint on trim,** which scuffs and does not wipe clean.

## When to skip painting

These are our judgment calls, not sourced rules.

- **The trim is only dirty.** Wash it and see. Painting over grime wastes the job.
- **You are only repainting the walls.** Tape or edge carefully and leave the trim alone.
- **The boards are damaged or very worn.** Replacing may beat endless filler, though that is a separate cost.
- **You rent.** Check the lease first. Our [security deposit guide](/blog/get-your-full-security-deposit-back) explains the common deductions, and our [renter-friendly upgrades list](/blog/renter-friendly-upgrades-with-prices) has options that do not need permission.

## Ways to save

- **Bundle trim with a wall job.** CountBricks lists bundling projects as a way to save, so ask for a combined quote.
- **Buy a quart.** There is no reason to buy a gallon for one room.
- **Do the prep.** Caulk, fill and sand before the painter arrives.
- **Get quotes that itemize.** Ask for baseboards, casing and each door as separate lines.
- **Consider cabinets separately.** They are priced very differently; see [kitchen cabinet painting costs](/blog/cost-to-paint-kitchen-cabinets).

## Trim and baseboard painting FAQ

### How much does it cost to paint baseboards per linear foot?
The sources we read put hiring a painter at about $1.00 to $3.50 per linear foot (CountBricks, February 2026) up to $2.30 to $4.88 (Homewyse, May 2026). Detailed profiles, heavy prep and small jobs sit toward the high end. A typical bedroom has about 45 feet, so roughly $45 to $220 by our arithmetic.

### How much does it cost to paint an interior door?
Fixr's figure is $150 on average for a standard door, with a typical range of $70 to $200. CountBricks gives $75 to $150 per door. Panel, louvered, glass and French doors cost more. If the frame and casing are billed separately, add $1 to $4 per linear foot (Fixr casing rate).

### Should I paint the trim or the walls first?
Either order works, and sources split. Trim first makes cutting in the walls cleaner and taping trim is faster; walls first is more forgiving for beginners. Whatever you choose, caulk and prep first, and plan a final touch-up on the trim.

### What paint and sheen should I use on baseboards?
Semi-gloss is the standard sheen, because it wipes clean and shows trim detail. Bob Vila's best-paint-for-trim page recommends oil-based paint, while its how-to-paint-baseboards guide recommends semi-gloss latex (water-based). Use primer on bare, stained or dark trim, and avoid flat or matte finishes.

### How long does it take to paint baseboards?
Bob Vila says a typical room takes under a day, and at least two days if you are new to painting or the boards need significant prep. Allow drying time between coats, and note that Fixr estimates 3 to 5 hours for one interior door.

### Do I need to remove baseboards to paint them?
No. Bob Vila advises against removing them just to paint, because pulling off caulked baseboards often damages the wall. Tape the wall and floor edges, use a paint guard, and brush carefully. Remove them only if you must.
