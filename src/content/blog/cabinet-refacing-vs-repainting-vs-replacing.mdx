Tired kitchen cabinets get three standard fixes: paint or refinish what you have, put new doors and veneer on the existing boxes (refacing), or tear everything out and install new cabinets. Published 2026 guides put a professional paint job at roughly $1,500–$5,000, refacing at $4,000–$12,000, and replacement anywhere from about $2,000 for a small stock kitchen to $30,000 or more for custom work, before countertops.

Those ranges overlap, and the sources disagree, partly because they quietly assume kitchens of different sizes. This guide lines the numbers up, shows where they stop matching, and gives decision rules based on what is actually wrong with your cabinets.

<Callout type="takeaway">
- **Painting or refinishing (pro):** about $1,500–$5,000, done in days. Same door style, same layout. Full detail is in our [kitchen cabinet painting cost guide](/blog/cost-to-paint-kitchen-cabinets).
- **Refacing:** about $4,000–$12,000, or roughly $100–$450 per linear foot depending on material. New doors, drawer fronts and veneer on the same boxes, typically a few days to a week on site.
- **Replacing:** about $2,000–$6,000 stock, $5,000–$13,000 semi-custom and $10,000–$32,000 custom for a 20–40 linear foot kitchen, plus removal, countertops and any plumbing or electrical changes.
- **Only replacement can change the layout.** Painting and refacing both need boxes that are structurally sound.
</Callout>

## What each option actually changes

| | Painting / refinishing | Refacing | Replacing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Existing boxes | Kept | Kept | Removed |
| Doors and drawer fronts | Kept, new finish | Replaced | Replaced |
| Box surfaces | Refinished | Covered in new veneer or laminate | New |
| Door style can change | No | Yes | Yes |
| Layout can change | No | No | Yes |
| Damaged boxes acceptable | No | No | Yes |

Painting changes color only. One manufacturer's comparison notes that paint keeps the original look, grain and flaws included, while refacing gives a new one. Refacing swaps the visible parts, so you can move from raised panels to flat fronts, but the interiors stay as they are and the boxes stay where they are. Replacement is the only route that lets you move a sink, add an island or rethink storage.

Sources do not agree on painting. Most 2026 pages land between $1,500 and $5,000: one guide prices a 20-door kitchen at $1,600–$5,000 with a $3,100 average, another cites $1,500–$7,000, and a third calls it $5,000–$9,000. The sources do not explain the gap. Our inference is that it comes from finish type (spray-applied factory-style finishes cost more than brushed paint), how many doors and drawers are counted, and the region.

Refacing is steadier. National figures run $4,500–$10,546 (Bob Vila, updated 2024), $4,000–$12,000 (CostToRenovate, March 2026) and $3,000–$13,500 with an $8,250 average (Fixr, January 2026). Per linear foot, Fixr gives $100–$450 by material: laminate $80–$125, wood veneer $100–$250 and solid wood $200–$500.

Replacement has the widest spread. Per linear foot, guides give stock at $80–$400, semi-custom at $150–$900 and custom at $500–$1,200 or more. Many of these pages come from companies that sell one of the three options, so treat their comparisons as starting points and get your own quotes.

## The linear-foot problem: how big is your kitchen?

Every per-foot price depends on how many linear feet the writer assumed, and they do not agree.

| Source | Kitchen assumed | Linear feet |
|---|---|---|
| Mainline Kitchen Design | Industry-standard 10×10 L-shape | 20 |
| CostToRenovate | Reference projects | 20–25 |
| Truvine | Medium kitchen | 20–30 |
| Bob Vila | Average full-size kitchen | 30 |
| House Digest | 200 sq ft kitchen | 30 |
| SimplyWise | A "10×10" kitchen | 40 |
| CostToRenovate | Calculator default | 60 |

The same "10×10 kitchen" label produces different totals. TC Wholesale's 10×10 figures ($2,000–$6,000 stock) match its per-foot rates multiplied by 20 feet. SimplyWise's ($3,200–$4,800 stock) match its rates multiplied by 40. One design firm also argues that the industry's 10×10 benchmark is a stripped-down layout and that real kitchens with moldings, drawer bases and organizers cost closer to double the per-foot price. That is one company's view, but it is a good reason to price your own kitchen.

Measure your own wall runs, uppers and lowers counted separately, and check that a quote's linear-foot count matches.

**Our arithmetic at 25 linear feet** (a middle assumption of ours, using per-foot rates from two sources):

| Option | Rates from SimplyWise | Rates from other guides |
|---|---|---|
| Refacing | $3,000–$6,250 | $2,500–$11,250 (Fixr) |
| Stock | $2,000–$3,000 | $2,500–$7,500 (TC Wholesale) |
| Semi-custom | $3,750–$7,500 | $6,250–$16,250 (TC Wholesale) |
| Custom | $12,500–$20,000 | $12,500–$30,000 (TC Wholesale) |

These are our multiplications, not quotes. They show how wide the honest answer is.

## Refacing is not always cheaper than stock cabinets

Most articles say refacing costs 30–50% less than replacing, and that holds against custom or semi-custom cabinets. It gets shaky against stock. On SimplyWise's numbers, a 40-foot kitchen costs $4,800–$10,000 to reface and $3,200–$4,800 to buy stock cabinets. TC Wholesale's stock range ($100–$300 per foot) overlaps refacing too.

So on the cabinet line alone, stock replacement can undercut refacing. The gap usually reverses once you add the costs that only replacement triggers, which is our inference from the lists below: removal, countertop work, patching and painting walls, and any moved plumbing. Refacing avoids all of that. If a stock quote and a refacing quote land close, compare the full projects and the box quality you would be left with.

## Hidden costs by option

| Cost | Painting | Refacing | Replacing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hardware | Reuse or buy new | Sometimes extra | Extra: $4–$25 per piece, 35–45 pieces typical |
| Removal and disposal | None | None | About $180–$1,000 across guides |
| Countertops | Untouched | Untouched | $1,800–$4,400 installed average, more for quartz |
| Plumbing or electrical moves | None | None | Electrical $500–$2,000, plumbing from about $1,000 |
| Permits | None | None | Roughly $460–$2,770 if you move utilities |
| Extras | Repairs and primer | Drawer boxes, pull-outs $50–$800 each | Fillers, trim, backsplash and wall paint $500–$1,500 |
| Surprises | Few | Few | Budget 10–15% for what the walls hide |

Our arithmetic on hardware: 35–45 pieces at $4–$25 is roughly $140–$1,125, which is why a hardware refresh is worth pricing separately on every option.

Replacing base cabinets usually means lifting the countertop, so plan on a new one. That is our inference, not something every source says outright, but the guides do list countertops as a separate cost. ## Timeline and lifespan

| | Painting | Refacing | Replacing |
|---|---|---|---|
| On site | Roughly 3–7 days | 1–7 days depending on the guide | 3–12 weeks depending on the guide |
| Kitchen usable | Mostly | Less disruptive than a tear-out | No, not during demolition and install |
| Lead time | Short | About three weeks including ordering (Fixr) | Custom: 6–8 weeks to build (TC) or 12–20 weeks (SimplyWise) |
| Expected life | About 5–15 years | About 10–20 years | About 20–30 years |

Refacing durations differ a lot: one company says 1–3 days, another 3–5, another 3–7 by kitchen size, another about a week. Lifespans come from a handful of guides. Painted finishes lasted 8–10 years in one and 7–12 in another, with gel stain at 5–8 and DIY finishes at 3–5. Refacing came in at 10–20 (Fixr), 10–15 and 15–20 (two others). New cabinets showed 20–30 years in two guides. We did not find dependable resale-value data. A few pages quote return percentages without methods, so we left them out.

## Choose painting if...

- The boxes and doors are structurally sound and you like the door style.
- Color or a worn finish is the only complaint.
- You want the lowest cost and shortest disruption. For prep, paint types and DIY numbers, see the [kitchen cabinet painting guide](/blog/cost-to-paint-kitchen-cabinets).

## Choose refacing if...

- The boxes are solid and the layout works, but the door style or damaged door faces bother you.
- You want new door styles, drawer fronts or hardware without moving plumbing.
- You want less mess than a tear-out and are willing to pay several times the paint price.

## Choose replacing if...

- Boxes show water damage, warping, mold or soft spots. The sources that address box condition agree that new veneer and doors need sound boxes to attach to.
- You want a different layout, more storage or an island.
- You are staying long term and want the longest lifespan.

## A simple decision flow

1. **Are the boxes damaged or the layout wrong?** If yes, replace. Neither painting nor refacing fixes it.
2. **Is the style fine and only the color or finish worn?** Paint.
3. **Does the door style itself feel dated or damaged?** Reface, then compare with a stock replacement quote.
4. **Is your budget under about $5,000?** Painting fits, and stock replacement might in a small kitchen. Refacing usually does not.

<Callout type="warning" title="Get quotes with the same scope">
Ask every contractor to state linear feet or door count, materials, hardware, installation, disposal and warranty. Price gaps between quotes often trace back to scope, not to how good the work is.
</Callout>

## Ways to save

- **Reuse the layout.** Electrical moves run about $500–$2,000 and plumbing moves start near $1,000, on top of the cabinets.
- **Choose laminate or thermofoil for refacing.** Laminate is the low end of the per-foot range; real wood veneer and solid wood cost up to about five times as much.
- **Consider a hybrid (our suggestion, not a sourced figure).** Paint sound boxes, or replace only the run that has to move, and ask for a separate price for each.

## Cabinet refacing, painting and replacing FAQ

### Is refacing cheaper than replacing cabinets?
Usually, yes. Guides say refacing costs 30–50% less than replacing, or about half. Against stock cabinets the gap can close or reverse on the cabinet line alone, but replacement adds removal, countertop and wall costs that refacing avoids.

### Is refacing worth it compared with painting?
It is worth it if the door style bothers you or the doors are worn. Painting changes only color and costs a fraction of refacing. If the style is acceptable and the boxes are sound, painting delivers most of the visual change for far less.

### Do I need a permit to replace kitchen cabinets?
Often not, if you leave plumbing, electrical and walls alone. One remodeler calls a like-for-like swap cosmetic in most areas. Moving a sink or adding circuits usually requires permits. Rules vary locally, so check with your building department. This is not legal advice.

### How many linear feet of cabinets does a typical kitchen have?
Sources disagree. The industry's 10×10 benchmark uses 20 feet. Guides describe medium kitchens at 20–30, a 200-square-foot kitchen at 30, and one calls a 10×10 kitchen 40. Measure your own runs, uppers and lowers counted separately, and compare quotes on that number.

### Do I have to replace countertops when I replace cabinets?
Not always, but often. Guides list countertops as a separate cost, and taking out base cabinets can disturb them. Our inference: budget for a new countertop unless your installer confirms yours can be lifted and reset.
