Move-out cleaning is the last chance to protect your deposit, which makes this one of the few places where paying more can be the financially smarter choice.

## Cost breakdown

For an average one-bedroom apartment:

| | DIY | Professional move-out cleaning |
|---|---|---|
| Supplies (if you don't already own them) | $30–$60 | Included |
| Time required | 4–8 hours | 2–4 hours (their time, not yours) |
| Typical cost | $30–$60 (supplies) + your time | $150–$350 |

## What professional move-out cleaners cover that most DIY cleans miss

- Inside oven and inside refrigerator (the two most commonly flagged items on move-out inspections)
- Behind/under large appliances
- Window tracks and blinds
- Baseboards and light fixture covers

These are exactly the spots landlords check first because they're the easiest to neglect — and each one can trigger a $25–$75 cleaning deduction on its own if missed.

## When DIY is the right call

- You have the time and the apartment is in good condition already (no heavy grease buildup, no pet hair, no long-term stains).
- Your lease/landlord has a reasonable, specific cleaning checklist you can follow item by item.

## When hiring pays for itself

- You're short on time during an already-stressful move.
- The unit has visible buildup in hard spots (oven, grout, exhaust fan) that would take hours to do properly yourself.
- Do the math: if a missed spot risks a $100+ deduction and professional cleaning costs $150–$350 total covering everything, the gap is smaller than it looks once you weigh the deduction risk.

## Bottom line

DIY cleaning saves money only if you're thorough about the specific spots landlords check first — oven, fridge, windows, baseboards. If you're rushed or the unit has real buildup, professional cleaning often pays for itself by preventing deductions, on top of saving your time during a move.
