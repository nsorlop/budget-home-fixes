Most deposit deductions come from a handful of predictable causes. Here's a checklist ranked by real risk level, based on the cost breakdowns across our other guides.

## High risk (commonly deducted)

| Issue | Typical deduction |
|---|---|
| Nail/screw holes left unpatched | $10–$25 per hole |
| Full room repainted a non-neutral color, left as-is | $150–$400 (cost to repaint back) |
| Permanent fixtures installed without approval (shelving, light fixtures) | $100–$600 (removal + repair) |
| Carpet stains or burns | $75–$300 per affected area |

## Medium risk (depends on execution)

| Issue | Risk driver |
|---|---|
| Removable wallpaper/backsplash on rough or poorly-painted walls | Adhesive can pull paint on bad surfaces — test a small spot first |
| Command-style hooks used beyond their weight rating | Failure can mark or dent the wall |
| DIY caulk/sealant work in bathrooms | Sloppy removal at move-out can damage tile finish |

## Low/no risk

- Adhesive hooks and hangers used within their rated weight
- Tension rods (curtains, closet systems)
- Freestanding furniture and shelving
- Removable wallpaper/film on properly primed, smooth walls, removed carefully

## The one habit that prevents most disputes

Take dated photos of every room on move-in day, including existing scuffs, nail holes, and wear — before you change anything. This is the single biggest factor in deposit disputes: without a move-in baseline, landlords can attribute pre-existing wear to you.

## Bottom line

The upgrades that put your deposit at risk are almost always the ones that require a hole, a permanent fixture, or a paint color change — not the reversible, adhesive-based options covered in our [renter-friendly upgrades guide](/blog/renter-friendly-upgrades-with-prices). When in doubt, document before you decorate.
