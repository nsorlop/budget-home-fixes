Covering an ugly rental floor costs anywhere from about $30 for a rug and pad to more than $1,600 for a room of premium carpet tiles, and the price gap matters less than what each product does to the floor underneath. On September 2026 retail listings, peel-and-stick vinyl tile ran roughly $0.85 to $1.53 per square foot and interlocking foam about $0.69 to $3.12. Click-together vinyl plank ran $2 to $7 in cost guides.

This guide compares six do-it-yourself ways to cover a floor, with a professional install as the yardstick. We assume a 120 sq ft room (12 by 10 feet), label our own arithmetic, and did not test any product. The risks cluster in adhesive residue, stain-prone rug backings and pads, and moisture trapped under solid covers.

<Callout type="takeaway">
- **One rug plus pad:** about $27 to $214 for a 5x7 rug and pad (our sum), which covers roughly 35 of the room's 120 sq ft.
- **Whole room, DIY:** about $85 to $375 for peel-and-stick tile, self-stick carpet tiles or foam. Click-together vinyl plank runs about $240 to $840. Tile-to-tile carpet tiles from FLOR run about $720 to $1,620.
- **Professional vinyl plank or laminate:** roughly $420 to $2,400 for the same room, depending on the guide.
- **Highest residue risk:** peel-and-stick tile. The instructions we read call for primer and rule out hardwood, carpet and floating floors.
- **Rug backings:** Floor & Decor says rubber or latex-backed rugs should not go on any vinyl floor.
</Callout>

## Where the sources disagree

- **Redfin versus 2026 listings.** A 2022 Redfin article gives $8 to $20 per sq ft (labor plus materials) for stick-on tiles, carpet tiles, click-in planks and foam alike. The 2026 listings we read for tiles and foam are $0.69 to $3.12 for materials alone. Redfin does not explain the gap; our inference is that it applies a professional-install figure to DIY products.
- **FLOR's price.** FLOR's own site listed tiles at $15.99 to $36 each. A third-party guide says $6 to $22 without saying why. We trust the manufacturer's dated page.
- **Professional labor.** Today's Homeowner says $1.50 to $3 per sq ft for vinyl plank labor (April 2025). This Old House says $3 to $10 (June 2026). Neither explains its range.
- **Carpet.** Redfin says stick-on tiles can go straight onto carpet. FLOR, a click-lock vinyl maker and a peel-and-stick maker say not to.
- **Natural rubber.** One pad guide calls natural rubber plus felt safe on hardwood, while Floor & Decor and RugPadUSA say rubber stays off vinyl. The floor type decides.

## What each option does to your floor

### Area rugs and pads

A rug needs no install and, in a 2026 tenant guide, counts as decorating that needs no landlord permission. The risk is underneath. Floor & Decor says neither natural nor synthetic rubber or latex backing belongs on any vinyl floor because it can discolor or stain it. RugPadUSA says heat from sun or foot friction sets off the reaction and recommends an EVA pad. A click-lock vinyl maker says the same about rubber-backed rugs.

On hardwood, a Weles guide says PVC and vinyl pads leach plasticizers that can yellow the finish, and trapped moisture can cloud it or warp boards. It says the damage builds slowly and often goes unnoticed until it is severe, which a two-year lease is long enough to allow. Felt without adhesive backing is the safest pad in the sources we read.

### Peel-and-stick vinyl tile

This is the cheapest way to cover a whole room, and the hardest to reverse. We read a Style Selections (Lowe's) instruction sheet, revised April 2023. It requires two coats of latex primer on the substrate, lists floating floors, hardwood, carpet, cushion vinyl, cork and foam as unsuitable, and says not to install over adhesive residue. New concrete must be at least 28 days old and dry, and the room must stay at 60 to 80 degrees F for 48 hours before, during and after. Tile floors need grout lines filled first.

Hunker says sticky residue stays on the subfloor after removal and older tiles get stickier. Our inference: primer plus adhesive makes this a permanent change on most rental floors, so treat it as a written-permission project, or use it only where the landlord plans to replace the floor anyway.

### Carpet tiles

There are two kinds. Self-stick tiles, such as the Foss Floors listings at Target, bond to the floor, so we expect the same residue problem as vinyl tile (our inference). FLOR uses small adhesive dots that join tile to tile. FLOR says that leaves no residue and no floor damage, and lists hardwood, tile, vinyl, laminate and concrete cured at least 90 days as suitable, but not carpet or padding. Its tiles are 19.7 inches square, 1/4 to 1/2 inch thick, and you cut the dots with scissors or a knife to lift one. It suggests ordering 10% extra. Those are manufacturer claims we did not test.

A stained tile can be swapped alone, which two guides list as a plus.

### Click-together and loose-lay vinyl plank

Click-lock planks float: they connect to each other, not the floor. A rigid-core maker's May 2023 instructions allow concrete, wood and existing vinyl or linoleum, but not cushioned or carpeted floors. The surface must be flat within 3/16 inch over 10 feet, and a 1/4 inch gap is required at walls and fixed objects. Concrete moisture must stay under 3 pounds per 1,000 sq ft per 24 hours, and rubber-backed rugs are off-limits. A 2026 guide says loose-lay plank grips by weight and friction, leaves no residue and suits vinyl, linoleum, concrete and tile, and that click-lock needs careful disassembly to reuse.

Our inference: the wall gap has to be hidden by baseboard or trim, so ask before you pull or nail anything.

### Interlocking foam and rigid tiles

Foam is cheap and quick. One guide says it is soft and warm underfoot and dampens sound, but dents under heavy furniture and is not waterproof at the seams. A Greatmats page adds that dents may not fully recover, suggests furniture coasters, and warns moisture can get trapped underneath in damp spaces. Its 7/16 inch tiles go over low-pile carpet only if there is no padding and the install is wall to wall. We found no manufacturer statement about hardwood. Rigid interlocking tiles ($3 to $8 per sq ft in that guide) are durable and reusable but can sound hollow and show seams.

## Deposit risk and permission

The 2026 tenant guide we read says rugs need no permission but alterations such as adhesive wallpaper usually do, and that leases can require restoring the original condition. State law varies, and this is not legal advice. Our own ratings, drawn from the instructions above: **low** for rugs with the right pad and for tile-to-tile carpet tiles; **low to medium** for foam and loose-lay plank; **medium** for click-lock if trim is involved; **high** for peel-and-stick tile, self-stick carpet tile and anything glued.

## Which option to choose

- **A rug** if the floor is fine and you want warmth, or you move often. Check the pad against your floor type.
- **Tile-to-tile carpet tiles** if you want wall-to-wall softness and can pay $720 to $1,620 for a room.
- **Loose-lay or click plank** on hard vinyl, tile or concrete, especially in wet-prone rooms, once the landlord agrees.
- **Foam** for a short stay or a play area, with coasters under furniture.
- **Peel-and-stick tile** only with written permission, or over a floor headed for replacement.
- **A professional install** only if the landlord pays or approves it. At $420 to $2,400 it does not move with you.

## Common mistakes

- Buying a rug pad on price without checking its material against your floor.
- Treating peel-and-stick as temporary. The residue and primer instructions say otherwise.
- Floating a plank floor over carpet or a cushioned floor, which the click-lock instructions we read rule out.
- Leaving foam or rugs untouched for months over hardwood or damp concrete.
- Comparing a rug's price with a full-room price without adjusting for coverage.

## Renter flooring FAQ

### What is the cheapest way to cover an ugly rental floor?
A rug and pad, at about $27 to $214 for a 5x7 set in September 2026 listings, is cheapest but covers only part of a room. For full coverage, peel-and-stick tile and foam tiles start around $0.70 to $0.85 per sq ft, though peel-and-stick carries the most residue risk.

### Can I put peel-and-stick tile over hardwood?
The manufacturer instructions we read list hardwood, carpet and floating floors as unsuitable, and say installing over an existing covering releases the maker from responsibility. They also require primer on the surface. Skip it unless your landlord approves the change in writing.

### Do rugs damage vinyl or hardwood?
They can. Floor & Decor says rubber or latex backings can permanently stain vinyl. A rug-pad guide says PVC and vinyl pads can yellow hardwood finishes over time, and that the damage is slow and easy to miss. Felt pads are the safest choice in the sources we read.

### Is click-together vinyl plank really removable?
It is not glued, so it lifts. A 2026 guide says click-lock needs careful disassembly if you want to reuse it. It also needs a flat floor and a 1/4 inch gap at the walls, so ask your landlord about baseboards and trim before you buy.

### Do I need my landlord's permission?
For a rug, a 2026 tenant guide says usually not. For adhesive products or anything that hides the whole floor, check your lease and ask in writing, since leases can require you to restore the original condition. Rules vary by state, and this is not legal advice.
